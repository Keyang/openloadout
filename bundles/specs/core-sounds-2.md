# Core sounds, part 2: specification

Written 2026-11-19 for the asset plan (`docs/asset-plan.md`, section 2: core libraries, the lines "Menu sound sets in 5 house styles, 8 sounds each" and "Weather and place loops"). These sounds belong to no single kit: any bundle may use them. 42 sounds in 8 batches (S01 to S08): four menu sound sets of eight (32) and ten place loops.

New sounds follow `../../skills/sprite-forge/SOUND.md` (the skill is at the workspace root) and `docs/style-guide.md` sections 11 and 13. Where this page and the style guide disagree, this page wins; where this page is silent, the style guide and the skill decide. The format and the general rules of `bundles/specs/core-sounds-1.md` ("What is different about core sounds") apply here too, except where this page says otherwise.

## Rules for every sound on this page

- **Do not duplicate** an existing sound in `sounds/` (read the list first: 105 files when this was written). Each row names **one** nearest existing sound; read that file first. Yours must be clearly different from it in what it is, not only in settings, and the row says how.
- **Controls are exact.** The row's control list is the patch's `params`, ids and labels as written, no more and no fewer. Every range written here is the range to build; narrow it only if an end is unusable, and say so in the report.
- **Styles are exact.** Names, capitals and all, because sprites cue them by name. A style may only set controls the row declares. Where styles differ in the kind of tone or in shape, the row gives a control for that (a choice control with `options`, or a stepped control): never fake a different kind of tone with Brightness alone.
- **Roles fit.** Menu sounds sit under `interface/`; the eighth sound of a set ("buy") is `interface/confirm`, because it is a menu button, not an item in the world. Place loops are `environment/weather` (rain) or `environment/ambience` (a place). A one-shot never takes an `environment/` role, and every loop here loops.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13): interface sounds are short and simple, so two sets can sound alike by accident. Each set below says what makes it its own; keep to it. No tune of two notes a fourth apart on a square wave, no rising major arpeggio ending on a held top note, no four-note rising "secret" figure. Say in the report, as steps up or down, the notes each sound plays.
- **Framework:** in this run, do **not** edit `sound/`, `quality/`, `tools/` or any file other than your own `sounds/<id>.json`. Several builders work at once. If the engine lacks something you need, make the nearest sound you can and say in the report what was missing. You therefore skip the fingerprint step of SOUND.md.
- **Scratch files.** Builders share one scratch folder, `out/`. **Name every scratch file with your sound id**: `out/<id>.png`, `out/<id>-<style>.png`, `out/<id>-try2.json`, `out/<id>.wav`. Never write a draft into `sounds/` (the build picks up every file there), never write a file without your id in its name, and never delete or overwrite a file you did not make.
- **Phone speakers:** at least 40% of the energy above 300 Hz at the default and in every style; measure it with the snippet in SOUND.md. The low loops (sea, city, machine room, underwater) are the ones at risk: give them a mid-range layer.

## Menu sound sets (S01 to S08)

### What a set is

A set is eight interface sounds with one **house voice**: the same instrument, the same room and the same pitch family, so a game can take the whole set and every button in its menus sounds like one object. The eight, with the same id ending, role and style names in every set:

| Sound | id ending | Role | Styles, exactly (the same in every set) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move (cursor to the next item) | `-move` | interface/hover | "List", "Grid", "Tab" |
| Confirm (choose) | `-confirm` | interface/confirm | "Select", "Accept", "Start game" |
| Back (cancel, leave a page) | `-back` | interface/click | "Back", "Cancel" |
| Error (not allowed) | `-error` | interface/error | "Not allowed", "Wrong answer" |
| Open (a panel or window appears) | `-open` | interface/click | "Menu", "Pop-up", "Inventory" |
| Close (it goes away) | `-close` | interface/click | "Menu", "Pop-up", "Inventory" |
| Toggle (a switch or checkbox) | `-toggle` | interface/click | "Switch on", "Switch off" |
| Buy or equip | `-buy` | interface/confirm | "Buy", "Equip" |

Because the style names are shared, a game switches its whole menu voice by changing only the id prefix (`menu-wood-confirm` to `menu-glass-confirm`, style "Accept" in both).

**All menu sounds:** type `sfx`, category `ui`, **identical every take** (no `rnd()`, no free-running `noise` or `dust` that differs by seed: variation 0.0), event at time 0 and sounding within 5 ms (open and close may swell to their loudest later, on purpose), died away by the end, not padded. `meta.themes`: `neutral` first, then at most one that truly fits the voice (wood: `fantasy`; glass: none or `fantasy`; console: none; bubble: `underwater`). `meta.gameTypes`: `arcade` first, then up to four that fit (`puzzle`, `card-game`, `turn-based-rpg`...). `meta.words`: 6 to 14 words, including the set's material (`wooden`, `glass`, `retro`, `bubble`) and the sound's job (`hover`, `select`, `cancel`, `toggle`, `shop`...).

**Shared pitch.** Every sound of a set has a `pitch` control (label Pitch, Hz, log) with **the same default and range** in all eight, and that pitch is the set's home note: the tunes are written relative to it. Then one setting moves the whole set together and it stays in tune with itself.

**Style rules.** Inside a set, styles never change the voice (material, room); they change use: higher, lower, shorter, longer, brighter. "Switch on" and "Switch off" differ in shape (up against down), so the toggle has its own `dir` control for it (lesson: shape gets a control). "Buy" and "Equip" differ in how many notes, so the buy sound has a stepped `notes` control.

**Not like these existing sounds.** No set may sound like `menu-move` (an unpitched dry tick over a short wooden knock), `menu-confirm` (two soft warm bell notes rising, glassy shimmer), `ui-click` (a pitched blip with a snap and an optional woody knock), `tech-click` (a pure tone with a metallic edge and a high tick) or `error-buzz` (two falling clashing buzzes). Nor may a set take one of the three kits' voices: chiptune (square, pulse, triangle, dry), storybook (soft sine and warm bells with a little room), or synthetic and gritty (saw, FM with grit, crush, dry).

Notes for every menu row:

- `length` is Length (s, log); `speed` is Speed (notes per second, its range chosen so the sound's length stays in the row's range); `ring` is Ring (s, log); `bright` is Brightness (0 to 1); `echo` 0.15 to 0.6 (never 0: the console set's repeat is heard at every setting, as the bubble set's pop and tail are at `bloop` 0); `bloop` 0 to 12 semitones (default about 7). Each `pitch` range is the set's, written in the set's first row.
- Where `speed` or `ring` sets the length, it is the sound's "how long" and there is no `length`.
- `dir` is a choice control (`"options": ["Off", "On"]`, `min` 0, `max` 1). The checker may warn that it changes the level by little: an up-or-down flip is heard as a change of shape, not loudness. Compare renders of both ends and say so in the report.
- Each set's style values (pitch, speed, length) should differ the same way in all eight sounds: "Grid" and "Pop-up" a little higher and shorter, "Tab", "Inventory" and "Start game" longer, "Wrong answer" lower and longer than "Not allowed".

## Place loops (S05 to S08)

**All loops:** type `ambience`, category `ambience`, `"loop": true`, duration 4 to 8 s (the row's), stereo where it helps, varies from take to take (`rnd()`, `noise`, `dust`). No single event so distinctive that the repeat is heard: an event that stands out (a drip, a creak, a wave) is varied in pitch and timing and happens at most a few times per loop. Any regular motion (a wave, a piston, a cricket's chirp) **completes whole cycles in the loop**. **Seam:** `"wrap": true` (as `alarm-siren` uses) crossfades the seam over only 8 ms in `sound/sfx-engine.js` `renderPatch`, so any slow random motion (`clock` to `rand` to `lag` swells and gates) jumps in level at the seam on every repeat. Only these loops set `"wrap": true`: those whose echo or reverb tails must cross the seam, row 36 `cave-drips` and row 42 `underwater` if it uses reverb, and the steady tonal drone of row 39 `machine-room` (a steady tone through the default 0.3 s crossfade swells or dips at every repeat). In a wrapped loop every tonal layer is either filtered noise or a tone held to whole cycles in the loop at every setting (multiples of 1/duration Hz: 0.25 Hz in 4 s, 0.125 Hz in 8 s), or its phase jumps at the seam on every repeat. In those loops every slow swell must be built from periodic motion that completes whole cycles in the loop, never from slow random motion. All other loops (including rows 33, 35, 37, 41, which have slow random motion) leave wrap off and get the default 0.3 s crossfade, as the existing noise loops (`wind`, `fire`, `bubbles`) do. `"normalize": false` for a loop whose loudness follows a control (the amount controls below), with a raw peak of 0.4 to 0.7 at the loudest setting and at most 0.95 everywhere. No volume control. `meta.themes`: `neutral` first only if the place has no world, otherwise the place (`forest`, `underwater`, `city`, `dungeon`...) then up to two more. `meta.gameTypes`: the kind of game that most needs the place first.

No plain wind (`wind` exists), no fire or hearth (`fire` exists), no bubbling pot (`bubbles` exists), no birds, frogs, voices or traffic horns (synthesis is poor at animals and speech; a horn is too distinctive to loop).

Notes for the loops:

- `bright` is Brightness (0 to 1) on every loop. "How long" is the loop length itself, so loops have no `length`. "How high" is `size` or `pitch` as the row says; `size` is always 0 to 1, with the default at 0.5 unless the style guide reasons otherwise.
- Choice controls (`surface`, `roof`, `beach`, `machine`) change the kind of tone, and the styles set them: "Attic" Tiles, "Tin shed" Tin, "Cabin" Wood; "Pebble beach" Pebbles, the other two Sand; "Boiler room" Boiler, "Server room" Fans, "Factory floor" Presses; for `rain`, "Drizzle" Leaves, "Steady rain" Ground, "Downpour" Puddles.
- `machine-room`'s Speed and `night-insects`' Speed must keep every rhythm a whole number of cycles in the loop at every setting: pick the rates from the loop length (4 s: 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 beats).
