# Hearthvale action-adventure kit: specification (`kit-adventure-hearthvale`)

Written 2026-11-21 by the art director for the asset plan (`docs/asset-plan.md` in `lab/sprite-forge/`, section 7, wave 1, row 51: "Hearthvale: top-down action-adventure", top-down). It is the second bundle of the pilot of kits that need nothing new from the framework; the first is Gridline (`kit-puzzle-gridline.md`). It goes with `kit-adventure-hearthvale.plan.json`; the two always agree. **Nothing new in it is built yet; the 40 reused sprites, 27 reused sounds and 3 reused tracks are in `sprites/` and `sounds/` today.** **Read `docs/style-guide.md` first** (clip names, frame counts, cue frames, control keys and labels, metadata, originality), then `docs/data-sprites.md` section 2 (the data format) and the skill (`.claude/skills/sprite-forge/SKILL.md`, `SOUND.md`). This file says what each asset is, and where it departs from the guide it says so and why. Where this page and the style guide disagree, this page wins for that asset; where this page is silent, the style guide and the skill decide.

## What is different about this kit

- **What the checklist asks for, and nothing of the look.** The reference game (plan, section 1) gives the list: a map walked screen by screen, dungeons with keys and a map, tools that open new places, hearts; and around them what any top-down game needs: a hero, townsfolk, enemies, bosses, ground, things to break, pickups, a few effects, menus, sounds for every act and six tracks. Everything here is drawn and named for Hearthvale. The originality rules for this kit are at the end of this list.
- **Reuse first: 40 of the 56 sprites, 27 of the 34 sounds and 3 of the 6 tracks exist already.** The plan's row counts 38 new sprites for an action-adventure; this kit makes 16, because the Hearthvale turn-based kit and the core libraries already hold the hero on the map, four villagers, four tilesets, the buildings, the chest, the door, hearts, keys, bombs, arrows and the menus. What is new is only what the reference game adds (things a tool opens: bushes, cracked walls, cave mouths, a block and a switch, a lit bomb; tool icons, item slots and a dungeon map) and what a hero fighting on the map needs (five enemies that are drawn top-down, and two bosses).
- **Every sprite is data, and no drawing code is added to the framework.** (One tool change, the kit-size rule, is listed under [New framework](#new-framework).) Each new sprite is one file `sprites/<id>.json` (`"format": "loadout-sprite", "version": 2`) with one body: six are `rig` bodies (two on a top-down person base, four with their own bones) and ten are `pictures` bodies. Each row names the base, the pieces and the generators it uses; **every one of them was checked to exist on 2026-11-21** with `node .claude/skills/sprite-forge/context.mjs generators` and `... pieces` (307 generators, 119 pieces). No row names a code helper, a code sprite or a generator that does not exist. Where a creature has no generator of its own (all five here, and both bosses), it is drawn from the format's own shapes (`ellipse`, `capsule`, `limb`, `poly`, `rect`, `line`, `arc`, `ring`, `stamp`: `docs/data-sprites.md` 2.7) on bones or nodes moved by clip keys. A maker who finds a generator or a piece missing stops and reports it; this kit has no helper batch for drawing code.
- **No tileset, no panel, no emitters and no layers body is new.** A batch is 3 to 6 sprites of one body kind; one new tileset or one new panel would be a batch of one. So the slot boxes and map marks are `pictures` bodies with their own `slice` and `textArea` written as data, and the caves use `tiles-dungeon` (its "Cobbles" style) with the new `cave-mouth` outside.
- **The kit is larger than any kit may be today, and that is not settled here.** `tools/lib/assets.mjs` holds every kit to `SMALL_KIT_SIZE` (12 to 50 sprites, 8 to 30 sounds, 1 to 4 tracks), and `tests/e2e/three-bundles.spec.mjs` fails a kit outside it. The plan gives this kit 56, 34 and 6. `node tools/bundles-from-plans.mjs --check --root .` accepts the plan (it prints only the assets still to come, and later a size note); the acceptance test will fail once the bundle is written, until a plan may carry its own size. This plan carries `"size": { "sprites": [50, 60], "sounds": [30, 36], "music": [4, 6] }`, which nothing reads yet. The change is the one item under [New framework](#new-framework), batch AK0 in the [Batch plan](#batch-plan): the Gridline kit's own unbuilt `kitSize(plan)` (its helper GH0), extended from puzzle plans to any plan that carries `size`; `SMALL_KIT_SIZE` is not raised for every kit; it needs the Loadout owner's yes and it blocks no asset batch, only the kit's acceptance.
- **Game type.** `catalog/schema.js` has no action-adventure game type; the plan (section 9) lists new game types as a framework addition for every wave. Until it exists the kit's `gameType` is `top-down-rpg`, and **every new asset's `gameTypes` starts with `top-down-rpg`**, then `turn-based-rpg` for map things a turn-based game can use unchanged, `roguelike-action` for enemies, hazards and room props, and `arcade` for interface and icons. Each row gives its list.
- **Themes:** `fantasy` first, then at most two of `forest`, `farm`, `dungeon`, `horror` as the row says. Interface: `fantasy`, `neutral`.
- **The player is `hero-walker`, as built.** It has the eleven clips of level "Full" (style guide, section 5) and a sword, an axe, a spear or bare hands. It has no bow, carry or push clip: a game plays `attack_*` with Weapon "none" (the punch) for using a tool, spawns `arrow` at its `hand` socket, and plays `walk_*` against a `push-block`. Nothing is added to it (`reusedChanges` is empty).
- **Deviations from the style guide, each with its reason; makers say so in the report:**
  - `cinder-acolyte`'s directed action is `cast_*` (section 5 names `attack_*` or `shoot_*`): it is a caster, and the top-down person base has `cast_down`, `cast_up` and `cast_side`.
  - `giant-mole` has no `walk`, `hop` or `fly` (section 4: every enemy has one way of moving): it moves under the ground, so `burrow` and `emerge` are its way of moving.
  - `spout-snail`'s clips have no direction suffix and are drawn facing right, as a `_side` clip is (section 5, rule 4: a clip with no suffix in a top-down sprite is drawn facing down and used for every direction): a snail has no readable front view (from the front the shell hides the body and the stalks), and the game mirrors it to face left.
  - `gloom-wisp` is a creature on a `pictures` body, not a `rig`: it is a flame with no limbs, drawn as light (no ink outline; rimmed with its own dark tone, section 2).
  - `gloom-wisp` has no `body` or `accent` colour (section 10: a creature always has both): it is a creature made only of light, so its colours are `core` ("Heart") and `glow` ("Flame"), as an effect's are.
  - `floor-switch` has pivot [16, 16] and `grounded: false` (a one-tile prop has [16, 30]): it lies flat in its tile like a pad, drawn as a plan (section 1 allows that for pads).
  - `cracked-wall` is 48x48 with pivot [24, 40] and draws a full 32x32 wall face in the middle of its frame (x 8 to 39, y 8 to 39), so a game lays it over a `wall-bottom` tile of `tiles-dungeon` and its rubble has 7 px of room on every side. A prop may not touch its frame's edge, which is why it is not 32x32. Its wall face has no ink outline (section 2 gives every prop one outline over the whole body): an ink ring round the face would land on the wall tiles beside it, so the outline op is limited to the group that holds the passage and the rubble (the `outline` op's `only`, docs/data-sprites.md). The one ink line inside the face is the wall tile's own foot line (tile row 29, y 37), copied as a stamp row of the face.
  - `cracked-wall` has no size control, no `style` choice and one material colour (section 10: a prop always has `size`, or `width` and `height`, and two colours): its face must match `tiles-dungeon`'s `wall-bottom` to the pixel. It has no `style` because the face must match the one wall tile: the tileset's `style` changes only its floor tiles and `wall-bottom` renders the same in all three, so three face options would draw one face, and a choice whose options change nothing is a checker error.
  - `ui-item-slots` is a 32x32 interface piece (a panel is 96x64): it is one slot, the size of an icon, with a 6 px nine-slice border so a game can stretch it.
  - `ui-dungeon-map` is a glyph set of 13 clips (a set has 6 to 10): a map needs rooms, doorways in two directions, locks and marks, and they must be one family.
  - `fused-bomb`, `cave-mouth`, `cut-bush`, `push-block` and `floor-switch` have no `wear` or no second colour where the row says so; each row lists its controls exactly.
- **Meaning stays fixed** (style guide section 3, rule 7): `crimson` is danger (the bomb about to blow, a boss room, locks); `heal` is healing; `magic` (cyan) is friendly light (a pressed switch, a set block, "you are here"); `gold` is reward; `hero` blue is the hero's own cloth and **no enemy offers `hero` as a body or outfit swatch**. Enemy lights are `ice`, `fire`, `mage` or `thunder`, never `magic` by default.
- **Colours are written as palette names.** `hearthvale.cobble` in this page is `PALETTE.hearthvale.cobble`; in the file a slot's `default` and its `swatches` are the hex values of those names (`docs/data-sprites.md` 2.1), in the order given. A swatch list written "exactly" is the whole list. A colour that is not a control (a tusk, a rivet, a rope) is a palette colour by name in a slot with no control, or a `derive` of another slot; never a typed shadow. A slot's `material` is one of the engine's sixteen (`MATERIAL_IDS` in `engine/materials.js`: warning, signal, skin, fire, sky, water, plant, earth, wood, light, magic, stone, metal, cloth, interface, paint); where a row gives none, the label decides it as the engine does.
- **Touching colour controls never share a swatch, and every list holds 4 to 8.** Each row says which lists touch. One exception: a light colour (`glow`: light drawn after the outline with its own dark rim, or the mole's crystals, each carried by its shard's `light` edge) may share one swatch with a list it touches if no version sets the two equal. Three rows use it, each with `mage`: `cinder-acolyte` (Fire and Robe), `barrow-warden` (Flame and Cloth) and `giant-mole` (Crystals and Fur). Two materials that touch differ in brightness at their defaults by 0.12 or more, with one accepted dark pair: `vale-brigand`'s Tunic (`bandit`, 0.333) and its Hood, cape and boots (`wood`, 0.452) at 0.119, the pair `road-bandit` already has (its row says why); makers check it under Mono. Makers check Sunset, Night, Handheld and Mono (`tools/render.mjs`, `tools/scene.mjs`).
- **Every slider states its default.** A slider is `"kind": "range"` running 0 to 1 with default 0.5 unless the row gives `min`, `max`, `step` and a default; a part's size or length is a multiplier 0.65 to 1.35, step 0.05, default 1. A slider does nothing by itself in a data sprite: the row says what it is bound to (a `scales` list, a binding table on a shape, a clip's `travel`). `speed` is always `{ "key": "speed", "standard": "speed" }`. Controls the row marks **Motion** carry `"group": "Motion"`; a part's settings are listed in the part; no other control has a group.
- **`when`.** A control or a cue followed by **when** `{ ... }` is in effect only for those values; copy it exactly. A toggle written "in every style" does something in every option of every choice and has no `when`.
- **Ready-made versions** (`docs/site-design.md` section 4). Every row lists 4 or 5 `versions`; the first is always the defaults (`params: {}`). Each must pass the checker and look different at card size on the preview clip from the defaults and from every other version. If one does not, change its settings, keep its name and say so in the report.
- **Sounds.** A new sprite cues only sounds that are in this kit's list of 34: the 27 reused (in `sounds/` today, with the styles named) or the 7 new (a sprite batch waits for the sound batch it cues). `idle` is silent except where a row gives a looping sound on a looping clip. Tiles, icons and map marks carry no cues.
- **Drawing styles (Contract 12).** Decision P5 holds: no row asks for the flat style. Every sprite is `"styles": ["pixel"]`, draws bodies with shapes and keeps single pixels and stamps for the detail layer the row names.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13 and the plan's section 1). Rules for this kit, on top of the guide's:
  1. **The hero stays the Hearthvale hero.** Blue tunic, red cape, plumed steel helmet (`hero-walker`). No pointed cap, no green tunic, no left-handed sword-and-shield pose, no elf ears. No version of any sprite here offers that look.
  2. **No famous item.** No boomerang, no chain hook fired from a handle, no flute or ocarina, no candle that burns bushes, no magic rod, no bottle with a fairy, no triangle emblem anywhere (not on a shield, a door, a block or a map). The eight tool icons are a compass, a horned key, a rope grapple, a gauntlet, a horn, a raft, an eye-stone amulet and an oil flask.
  3. **No famous creature.** No rock-spitting octopus, no spinning flower, no jumping one-eyed spider, no pig-faced spearman, no robed wizard that fades in and out, no white sheet ghost, no statue that wakes, no hand that drops from the ceiling. The five enemies are a hooded brigand, a fire acolyte who walks, a boar on four legs, a snail that spits and a blue flame; the bosses are an empty suit of armour with a fire where its face should be, and a giant mole.
  4. **Hearts are the library's.** `heart` and the hearts of `ui-hud-platformer` as built. No heart in a square frame as a "more health" prize: a game uses `heart` at Size "Big" for that.
  5. **No borrowed music or jingles.** No overworld march that opens with a leap up a fourth and a triplet run; no "secret" figure of eight quick rising notes; no rising four-note "item found" flourish (`item-get` and `secret-found` are the library's own). Every tune, chord order and rhythm passes `tests/unit/compose-library.test.mjs`.
  6. **No borrowed names.** "Tool", "item slot", "dungeon map", "compass", "boss door", "cracked wall", "push block": the genre's things, in plain words; never the reference game's names for places, people, items or enemies.

How to read a sprite row. **Meta:** type, role, view, themes, gameTypes, words, batch. **Frame:** size and pivot. **Body:** which of the six bodies, and what it starts from. **Clips:** name, frame count, "loop" if it loops, then the label in brackets where the everyday word differs; fps and what a standard clip must show are in the style guide, section 4, and what is special is written here. **Parts:** each part with its selector's options, its other settings and its socket. **Controls:** the sprite's own controls by key, in the order of the file, each with kind, label, ends or options, default, what it is bound to, and its `when`; standard keys take the style guide's label and end words (section 10). **Sounds:** clip, frame, sound id and style (the cue's `preset`), volume. **Versions:** name and `params`. **Uses:** every piece and generator the file names, with its version. **Drawing:** the detail layer. **Look and motion:** what a maker draws and a reviewer checks.

## The palette

"Hearthvale" as it stands (style guide, section 3; 30 colours). **The kit needs no colour the palette lacks, and no engine file changes.** The plan file lists the same colours as `kit-rpg-fantasy.plan.json` and `engine/palettes.js`; `bundles-from-plans` checks that. The bundle id is not in `BUNDLE_PALETTE` and does not need to be: a data sprite writes hex values.

What this kit leans on, with brightness (0 black to 1 white), because the new things must stand clear of the ground they are seen on:

| Ground or thing | Colour | Brightness |
|---|---|---|
| Meadow grass (`tiles-meadow`) | meadow | 0.63 |
| Paths and sand (`tiles-meadow`, `tiles-shore`) | path | 0.65 |
| Water (`tiles-shore`) | water | 0.58 |
| Village stone (`tiles-village`) | cobble | 0.65 |
| Dungeon floor (`tiles-dungeon`) | flagstone | 0.52 |
| Dungeon walls (`tiles-dungeon`) | slate | 0.38 |
| Dark bodies that read on every ground | bandit 0.33, slate 0.38, pine 0.38, mage 0.41, crimson 0.41, hero 0.42, rogue 0.45, wood 0.45 | |
| Light things that read on the dungeon floor | steel 0.79, wheat 0.78, cream 0.92, cobble 0.65 | |

So: a creature of the overworld takes a body darker than 0.50 (grass, sand and water are 0.58 to 0.65); a thing of the dungeon is either darker than 0.42 with a light second colour, or lighter than 0.64 (the floor is 0.52). Every default below follows that. No list offers `meadow` for something that stands on grass or `flagstone` for something that stands on the dungeon floor.

#### `stone-scrape` · Stone scrape

- **Meta:** type `sfx`, role `world/machine`, category `impact`, themes `fantasy`, `dungeon`. Batch **AS1**.
- **Length:** 0.25 to 0.9 s.
- **What makes the sound, and how it moves:** A heavy block dragged over a stone floor. Brown `noise` through a band-pass that wanders slowly between about 250 and 900 Hz (its centre driven by a `lag` of noise, so no two drags grind alike), with `dust` grit clicks through a second band-pass near 2 kHz for the phone range, both under one swell that rises in 20 ms and holds. At the end, when `thud` is above 0, a short stop: a sine falling from about 110 to 60 Hz in 80 ms with a `modal` stone knock near 320 Hz on top. **The three new tracks.** Each follows the Music section of `SOUND.md`, the style guide's section 12 (fixed control ids `intensity`, `bpm` (label Speed), `key` (label Pitch), `bars`, `inst`, `bright`, `space`; lengths 8, 16, 24, 32 bars; the five melody instruments in order) and `docs/music-styles.md`: each is written on one **band** with `band(name)` from `tools/compose/kit.mjs` (`B.bass`, `B.chords`, `B.drums`, `B.lead`, `B.room`, `B.defaults`, `B.tags`, `B.words`), with the tune on `melody('l', ...)` and `instruments('l', ...)` unchanged and the band's own lead on the answer line. Chords below are the chords the checker must find per bar. **No two tracks of the library share a chord order**: each order above was compared, counted from the first bar's root, with all 34 music loops in `sounds/` on 2026-11-21 (the test's own `chordOrder` was run over every file) and with each other, and is unique. The nearest are `hearthvale-village` and `gridline-rush`, which share the overworld's first four bars, `0M 7M 9m 5M`, and go to `0M` in bar 5 where the overworld goes to `2m`, and `gridline-calm`, which opens `0m 8M 5m 10M` where the dungeon has `0m 8M 5m 7M`. `tests/unit/compose-library.test.mjs` is run when each track lands; tunes and their rhythms must also pass it (no 7 notes in a row moving the same way as another track; at most one bar of the same rhythm).

**Listening gate** (`docs/music-styles.md`, status): no band is used for a track that ships until a person has listened to its probe. `folk`, `organ` and `marimba` each carry a menu and a credits theme already; whether a person has heard those probes is not recorded, so each music batch names it as a need.

Every fastest tempo plus 5% stays inside its band (style guide section 12; `sound/bands.js`): `folk` 84 to 132 holds 124 + 5% = 130.2; `organ` 64 to 140 holds 92 + 5% = 96.6; `marimba` 72 to 120 holds 104 + 5% = 109.2. **The tune is still playing at intensity 0.5** in every track.

#### `hearthvale-overworld` · Overworld theme

- **Role:** `music/exploration`. Batch **AM1**. **Band:** `folk`. **Use:** walking the map between the village and the dungeons. **Nearest generator:** `tools/compose/menu_folk.mjs` (the same band and its row layout); `tools/compose/hearthvale_village.mjs` for this world's tone.
- **Tempo:** 112 bpm by default, range 100 to 124. **Key:** D major. **Mood:** striding, open-air, hopeful: a road with a long way to go. Themes `fantasy`, `forest`.
- **Chords (8 bars, one per bar):** D, A, Bm, G, Em, D, F#m, A (I, V, vi, IV, ii, I, iii, V). Not D, A, Bm, F#m, G, D: those six bars in D are the opening of Pachelbel's Canon, a piece a player could name (style guide section 13), so bars 4, 5 and 7 are placed to keep clear of it.
- **Layers:** (1) Chords: the band's `strum` on the 8ths, down strokes on the beats and up strokes between (`B.chords`, with `dip` lowering it while the tune plays). (2) Bass: `roundBass`, half notes, root on 1 and fifth on 3 (its own `semis` row `0 _ _ _ _ _ _ _ 7 _ _ _ _ _ _ _`). (3) The tune (`instruments`). (4) Drums (`B.drums`): `hand` on 1 and the "and" of 2 and on 3, `shaker` in 8ths, `tambourine` on 2 and 4. (5) The band's `pluckedString` lead answering the tune with a 16th tremolo in bars 4 and 8.
- **Intensity:** 0 to 0.25: strum and bass. 0.25 to 0.5: add the tune and the shaker. 0.5 to 0.75: add the hand drum and the tambourine. 0.75 to 1: add the answer line, the strum an octave up as well.
- **Length:** 8, 16, 24 or 32 bars; default 16. Longer versions open with strum and bass, then the tune, then the drums, then everything.
- **Melody instrument:** the five standard choices; default "Flute".
- **Other controls:** `lead` (melody level, default 0.8) and `swing` (label "Lilt", default 0.2): 9 controls in all.
- **Styles:** "Setting out", "Open fields", "Far from home".
- **Melody:** a striding 2-bar phrase: it starts on F#4 (the third), walks up by steps in even 8ths to A4, holds B4 for a half note, and comes down by steps to D4; bars 3 and 4 say it a third higher and rest on D5 (or B4) over G: both are notes of bar 4's chord, where C#5 would be a sharp fourth and fail the checker, which needs two of a bar's three strongest notes in its chord. Bars 5 to 8 answer: one leap of an octave, E4 to E5 (the root of bar 5's Em; D is not in that chord), at the start of bar 5, then a long walk down by steps through bars 6 and 7 to end bar 8 on E4 over A, which leads back to the F#. Mostly steps and that one leap. **Not** a march: no opening leap of a fourth, no triplets, no dotted fanfare rhythm, no brass.

#### `hearthvale-dungeon` · Dungeon theme

- **Role:** `music/exploration`. Batch **AM2**. **Band:** `organ`. **Use:** the crypt's rooms. **Nearest generator:** `tools/compose/menu_organ.mjs` (the same band: pedal bass, held organ, the light kit).
- **Tempo:** 80 bpm by default, range 72 to 92. **Key:** E minor. **Mood:** cold, slow, watchful: stone, candle smoke, something further in. Themes `fantasy`, `dungeon`.
- **Chords (8 bars, one per bar):** Em, C, Am, B, Em, D, C, B (i, VI, iv, V, i, VII, VI, V).
- **Layers:** (1) Bass: `pedalBass`, a dotted half on the root and a quarter-note passing tone that leads to the next bar's root (its own `semis` row). (2) Chords: the band's `drawbarOrgan` held a bar each, low and quiet (`B.chords`, with `dip`). (3) The tune. (4) Drums (`B.drums`), none below intensity 0.5: then `ride` on the quarters and `kick` on 1; from 0.75 `snare` (brushed) on 3. (5) The band's organ lead through `rotary`, answering high in bars 4 and 8.
- **Intensity:** 0 to 0.3: held organ and pedal bass. 0.3 to 0.5: add the tune. 0.5 to 0.75: add ride and kick. 0.75 to 1: add the brushed snare and the answer line.
- **Length:** 8, 16, 24 or 32 bars; default 16. Longer versions: organ and bass, then the tune, then the kit, then everything.
- **Melody instrument:** the five standard choices; default "Bell".
- **Other controls:** `lead` (melody level, default 0.8): 8 controls in all. `space` defaults to the band's 0.35.
- **Styles:** "First steps", "Deeper", "Before the door".
- **Melody:** sparse, long notes with rests. A 2-bar cell: E4 up a minor third to G4, held (bar 1, Em); then G4 again and down a third to E4, held (bar 2, C). Bars 3 and 4 answer it: A4 up to C5, held (bar 3, Am); then B4, held (bar 4, B), so every held note is in its bar's chord (no F#4 over C: a sharp fourth, which fails the checker as the overworld's C#5 over G would). Bars 5 to 8 turn the cell upside down (B4 down to G4, up to A4), then fall by steps to end bar 8 on D#4, the leading note over B, so the loop wants to start again. No run of more than four notes; at least a quarter rest in every second bar.

#### `hearthvale-cave` · Cave theme

- **Role:** `music/exploration`. Batch **AM3**. **Band:** `marimba`. **Use:** caves and the mole's tunnels. **Nearest generator:** `tools/compose/menu_marimba.mjs` (the same band: bass bar rolls, broken chords, the wood kit).
- **Tempo:** 92 bpm by default, range 80 to 104. **Key:** B minor. **Mood:** damp, hollow, curious: drips, echoes, a lantern. Themes `fantasy`, `dungeon`.
- **Chords (8 bars, one per bar):** Bm, D, Em, Bm, G, D, Em, F# (i, III, iv, i, VI, III, iv, V).
- **Layers:** (1) Bass: `bassBar`, half notes played as 16th rolls, root then fifth (its own row). (2) Chords: the band's `marimba` in broken chords on the 8ths, low (`B.chords`, with `dip`). (3) The tune. (4) Drums (`B.drums`): `woodblock` like drips, on the "and" of 2 and on 4 of every bar and once more on the "and" of 3 in bars 4 and 8; `guiro` one scrape on beat 3 of every second bar; `log` on 1 from intensity 0.5. (5) The band's marimba lead echoing the last three notes of each 2-bar phrase an octave up.
- **Intensity:** 0 to 0.25: broken chords and woodblock. 0.25 to 0.5: add the bass and the tune. 0.5 to 0.75: add the log drum and the guiro. 0.75 to 1: add the echo line.
- **Length:** 8, 16, 24 or 32 bars; default 16. Longer versions: chords and woodblock, then bass and tune, then the wood kit, then everything.
- **Melody instrument:** the five standard choices; default "Plucked string".
- **Other controls:** `lead` (melody level, default 0.8) and `swing` (label "Bounce", default 0.25): 9 controls in all.
- **Styles:** "Dripping dark", "Lantern light", "Something moves".
- **Melody:** off the beat and hopping. **A hook that runs down the chord is asked for here:** bar 1 drops B4, F#4, D4 on the off-beats and steps back up to E4; bar 2 holds F#4 over D. Bars 3 and 4 run down E minor the same way (B4, G4, E4) and settle on D4 then B3. Bars 5 to 8 lift the hook a third (D5, B4, G4), walk down by steps over D and Em, and end on A#4 (the leading note) over F#. Its rhythm starts every phrase on the "and" of 1, which no other track's tune does.

## Left out, and why

- **A new hero with a shield, a bow and carry clips.** The plan's row counts five people and the library has them. `hero-walker` fights with what it has; a game plays `attack_*` for a tool and spawns `arrow` or `fused-bomb` itself. A second hero would be a new base's worth of clips (shoot with a bow, lift, push), which the framework does not hold: the top-down bases have no bow.
- **A third boss and three more creatures** (the row has 3 and 10; this kit has 2 and 7). Each new creature here is drawn from plain shapes with no generator behind it, the most expensive kind of sprite to make and review; two bosses and five enemies cover a first dungeon, a second dungeon and the land between.
- **New tilesets** (the row has 6; this kit has 4, all reused). The four Hearthvale tilesets cover meadow, shore, village and dungeon; the cave is `tiles-dungeon` in its "Cobbles" style with Damp on. A cliff and cave-floor tileset is the first thing a second Hearthvale adventure bundle should add; alone it would be a batch of one (tiles body).
- **A world map screen for the overworld.** `icons-map` (pins, flags, a town, treasure, "you are here") is in the core library for it; it is not one of the 56.
- **Portraits.** `portraits-village` exists for the four villagers and can be used with `ui-dialogue`; this kind of game shows no faces while it plays.
- **Magic and its meter.** The reference game's later tools (a rod, a meter of magic) are not on the first-hour checklist; `ui-bars-rpg` is there for a game that wants a meter.
- **A sword beam, a boomerang, a hook fired on a chain.** Famous items: see the originality rules.
- **Weather and light overlays.** `rainfall`, `mist-drift`, `fireflies`, `sun-rays` and `ember-drift` are in the core library and suit the map and the dungeons; a game adds them as it likes.
- **Footsteps on wood, sand and water.** In the core library (`footstep-wood`, `footstep-sand`, `footstep-water`); the kit lists the two surfaces every screen has.
- **The flat drawing style.** Decision P5 (the plan's section 5). Each row names its detail layer so it can follow.

## Batch plan

A batch starts when everything in its "Waits for" cell has passed review. Sounds first (a sprite cannot cue a sound that does not exist), then sprites, with music alongside. No batch adds to the framework. A sprite batch is 3 to 6 sprites of one body kind; no two batches write the same file; a sound batch is at most 6; a music batch is one track. The palette exists. Every reused asset is in `sprites/` or `sounds/` today: nothing waits on them.
