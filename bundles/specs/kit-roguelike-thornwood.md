# Thornwood side-view roguelike kit: specification (`kit-roguelike-thornwood`)

Written 2026-11-25 by the art director for the asset plan (`docs/asset-plan.md` in `lab/sprite-forge/`, section 7, wave 1, row 14: "Thornwood: side-view roguelike", side view). It is the fourth bundle of wave 1, after Gridline (`kit-puzzle-gridline.md`), Hearthvale (`kit-adventure-hearthvale.md`) and Cloudreach (`kit-platformer-cloudreach.md`). It goes with `kit-roguelike-thornwood.plan.json`; the two always agree. **Nothing new in it is built yet; the 36 reused sprites, 29 reused sounds and 3 reused tracks are in `sprites/` and `sounds/` today.** **Read `docs/style-guide.md` first** (clip names, frame counts, cue frames, control keys and labels, metadata, originality), then `docs/data-sprites.md` section 2 (the data format) and the skill (`../../skills/sprite-forge/SKILL.md`, `SOUND.md`). This file says what each asset is, and where it departs from the guide it says so and why. Where this page and the style guide disagree, this page wins for that asset; where this page is silent, the style guide and the skill decide.

## What is different about this kit

- **What the checklist asks for, and nothing of the look.** The reference game (plan, section 1) gives the list: fast melee and ranged weapons, upgrade scrolls, doors between areas, elite enemies, and a run that starts again from the beginning; and around them what any side-view action game needs: a hero, enemies and bosses, ground and platforms, traps, pickups, effects, backdrops, menus, sounds and five tracks. Everything here is drawn and named for Thornwood. The originality rules for this kit are at the end of this list.
- **Reuse first: 36 of the 48 sprites, 29 of the 34 sounds and 3 of the 5 tracks exist already.** The plan's row counts 34 new sprites for an action roguelike; this kit makes 12, because the Thornwood platformer kit and the core libraries already hold the ground of the forest, the caves and the keep, six enemies, two bosses, doors, chests, traps, torches, the scroll, the flask, coins and gems, arrows, hit effects, both backdrops and the wooden menus. New is only what the reference game adds that nothing in the library does: a hero who strikes, shoots and dodges; an enemy that shoots back; an elite; the gate between areas; the stand a scroll is read at; a weapon lying on the ground; a thrown knife; the marks of an elite, of a boon taken and of loot; the run's HUD glyphs; and the boon icons.
- **Every sprite is data, and no generator, piece, base or drawing code is added.** Each new sprite is one file `sprites/<id>.json` (`"format": "loadout-sprite", "version": 2`) with one body: three `rig` (all three on the side-view person base), six `pictures` and three `emitters`. Each row names the base, the pieces and the generators it uses; **every one was checked to exist on 2026-11-25** with `node ../../skills/sprite-forge/context.mjs generators` and `... pieces` (run with `--root` on this working copy: 310 generators, 119 pieces, all six bodies built). Where nothing fits (a bow, a great weapon, a gate), the sprite is drawn from the format's own shapes (`ellipse`, `capsule`, `limb`, `poly`, `rect`, `line`, `arc`, `ring`, `stamp`: `docs/data-sprites.md` 2.7). A maker who finds something missing stops that sprite and reports "blocked: <what is missing>".
- **No tileset, no backdrop and no panel is new.** The forest, the caves and the keep are `tiles-forest`, `tiles-cave`, `tiles-castle` and `tiles-platforms` in front of `bg-forest` and `bg-castle`. A second kit in a world and a view that exist reuses them (plan, section 6).
- **The palette exists; this kit adds no colour and takes no engine step.** The plan file declares the palette "Thornwood" with the engine's 23 colours, as `kit-platformer-fantasy.plan.json` does, so `node tools/bundles-from-plans.mjs --check --root .` finds the two in agreement. `BUNDLE_PALETTE` in `engine/palettes.js` gets no line for this kit: sprites name the palette by its key, `thornwood`, as the style guide asks.
- **The kit's size is its own.** 34 sounds and 5 tracks are above what a kit holds without saying so (`SMALL_KIT_SIZE`: 8 to 30 sounds, 1 to 4 tracks). The plan carries `"size": { "sprites": [44, 50], "sounds": [30, 36], "music": [4, 5] }`, which `kitSize` in `tools/lib/assets.mjs` reads today.
- **Themes:** `fantasy` first, then at most one of `forest` (forest things) or `dungeon` (keep things). Interface: `fantasy`, `neutral`.
- **gameTypes:** `roguelike-action` first on everything new; `platformer` on characters, props and effects a platformer can use unchanged; `turn-based-rpg` on icons; `arcade` on pickups and interface.
- **The player is `keep-runner`, new.** No library hero fits: `knight` strikes and blocks but neither shoots nor dodges; `rogue-battler` strikes and throws but has no dodge and its crouched stance is its own. `keep-runner` is on the side-view person base, holds a hand weapon and a bow at once, and has the dodge as its `dodge` clip (label "Dodge"): the playground plays a clip called `dodge` on its dash key and moves the character while it plays. The dodge is a low dash along the ground, the "streak" that the style guide's section 4 allows beside the tucked roll ("tucked roll or streak"), so it is inside the guide and not a deviation: the side-view person base has no tucked pose. Frame 1 is the crouch and frames 2 to 5 are one held stretched pose, as on `cloud-courier`, whose `dodge` keys are the start. `rogue-battler` stays in the kit as a second figure to play.
- **Deviations from the style guide, each with its reason; makers say so in the report:**
  - `keep-runner` has two held parts (section 9: one held and one worn): `weapon` on the socket `hand` and `bow` on the socket `offhand`. The reference game's addition is a fighter who strikes and shoots without changing what is in the hands.
  - `briar-archer` has an extra looping clip `aim` (label "Take aim") between `walk` and `shoot`: `shoot` is 6 frames and its one draw frame is too short a warning for a player to read, so the game holds `aim` first.
  - `briar-archer`'s `bow` part has no `none` (section 9: include `none` unless the specification says the part cannot be removed): an archer without a bow has no attack. `keep-champion`'s `weapon` has no `none` for the same reason.
  - `keep-champion` has no `outfit` control (section 10 lists `outfit` as standard for a character): it is armour from helm to boots, so `metal` ("Armour") takes the place of `outfit` and paints the base's top and bottom, as `bone-soldier` has no `outfit` either (its `body` paints the whole figure).
  - `keep-champion` is an enemy with eight clips and a second attack (`charge`) and a boss-like `rally`: an elite is read by having one move more than the common enemy. It is not a boss: its frame is the person's 64x64.
  - `area-gate`'s clips are `closed`, `open`, `sealed`, `unseal` (the guide's door order is `closed`, `open`, `locked`): a gate between areas is never opened with a key; it is either free or sealed until the area's elite falls. Stepped in order it reads: shut, rises open, (shut again and sealed), the seal breaks and it is shut and free.
  - `area-gate` is taller in its frame than section 1's "Fill the frame" asks (half to three quarters of the frame along its longer side): the arch is 62 to 70 px of an 80 px frame, 78 to 88%, and 66 px (83%) at the defaults. A gate that a figure of 35 to 46 px walks through needs a 44 px opening with masonry over it, and the next frame up (96x96) would leave it emptier, not fuller. No checker test measures the upper end; the maker says so in the report.
  - `ui-hud-keep` has no `size` control (section 10: an icon or glyph set always has `shine` and `size`), as `ui-hud-platformer` and `ui-hud-courier` have none: a HUD glyph is drawn at one size, 14x14 in its 16x16 cell, so that a row of flasks lines up; a game scales the whole HUD. It has no `speed`: its two animated clips have 4 frames and play at a fixed 8 fps (section 10).
  - `loot-beam` has `height` and `width` in place of `size` (section 10: an effect has `size`): a game fits the beam to what lies under it.
  - `elite-aura`, `scroll-surge` and `loot-beam` list `loop` or `burst` first as the row says; each row names its first clip, which is the one the checker measures.
- **Meaning stays fixed** (style guide section 3, rule 7): `crimson` is danger and the flask's healing draught is the library `potion`'s red; `gold` is reward (coins, the default loot beam); `magic` cyan is the keep's own friendly magic (the scroll's light, a gate's seal, a boon taken); `hero` blue is the runner's own cloth and **no enemy offers `hero` as a swatch**; `thorn` purple is the enemy accent; `ember` is the elite's fire. **A danger colour that no control paints is a fixed slot with `"material": "warning"`**, and a fixed light takes `"material": "light"` (style guide section 10, "Whole-bundle looks"); the rows say where.
- **Colours are written as palette names.** `thornwood.slate` in this page is the hex of `slate` in the table below; in the file a slot's `default` and its `swatches` are those hex values, in the order given. A swatch list written "exactly" is the whole list. A colour that is not a control is a palette colour by name in a slot with no control, or a `derive` of another slot; never a typed shadow. A slot's `material` is one of the engine's sixteen (`MATERIAL_IDS` in `engine/materials.js`); where a row gives none, the label decides it as the engine does.
- **Touching colour controls never share a swatch, every list holds 4 to 8, and two materials that touch differ in brightness by 0.12 or more at their defaults.** Each row says which lists touch and gives the figures. One exception, as in Hearthvale and Cloudreach: a light colour (drawn after the outline with its own dark rim) may lie nearer than that to what is under it where the row says so. Makers check Sunset, Night, Handheld and Mono (`tools/render.mjs`, `tools/scene.mjs`).
- **Every slider states its default.** A slider is `"kind": "range"` running 0 to 1 with default 0.5 unless the row gives `min`, `max`, `step` and a default; a part's size or length is a multiplier 0.65 to 1.35, step 0.05, default 1. A slider does nothing by itself in a data sprite: the row says what it is bound to (a `scales` list, a binding table on a shape or a generator argument, a clip's `travel`). `speed` is always `{ "key": "speed", "standard": "speed" }`. Controls the row marks **Motion** carry `"group": "Motion"`; a part's settings are listed in the part; no other control has a group.
- **`when`.** A control or a cue followed by **when** `{ ... }` is in effect only for those values; copy it exactly. A toggle written "in every style" has no `when`.
- **Ready-made versions.** Every row lists 4 or 5 `versions`; the first is always the defaults (`params: {}`). A name is at most four everyday words, repeats no word and is not a control's label. Each must pass the checker and look different at card size on the preview clip from every other. If one does not, change its settings, keep its name and say so in the report.
- **Sounds.** A new sprite cues only sounds in this kit's list of 34: the 29 reused (in `sounds/` today, with the styles named) or the 5 new (a sprite batch waits for the sound batch). `idle` is silent except where a row gives a looping sound on a looping clip. Glyphs and icons carry no cues. A clip of an interface sprite that carries no text says `"noText": true`; the one interface row says so for every clip.
- **Drawing styles (Contract 12).** Decision P5 holds: no row asks for the flat style. Every sprite is `"styles": ["pixel"]`, draws bodies with shapes and keeps single pixels and stamps for the detail layer the row names.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13 and the plan's section 1). Rules for this kit, on top of the guide's:
  1. **The hero is a person with a face.** A runner in blue with a headband, a hand weapon and a bow. No head made of flame or smoke, no head without a face, no ragged red scarf at the neck, no prisoner's clothes; no version offers that look. And not a green pointed cap and tunic with a sword (style guide, section 13): no version pairs a `leaf` jacket with the hood.
  2. **The currency is coins and gems** (`coin`, `gem`). Nothing glowing is pulled out of a fallen enemy and carried to a collector between areas.
  3. **Boons are not three colours.** The eight boon icons are eight things with eight outlines, all in one set of materials; no red, purple and green triad of stats, and no scroll that offers "one of three colours".
  4. **The gate is a portcullis in an arch with an emblem.** No door that counts down a time, no door with a number of bosses beaten on it, no door of bones.
  5. **The elite is a keep's champion in dark iron with an ember rune.** Not a bigger copy of a common enemy in another colour, no ring of orbiting lights, no crown of horns of light: the `elite-aura` is a ring of thorns on the floor.
  6. **The safe room has a fire and a keeper** (`campfire`, `wizard`). No hanging giant, no collector with a syringe, no forge that rerolls.
  7. **No borrowed music.** No flamenco guitar and no organ toccata as the run theme; the two new tunes are a chip tune in E minor with one borrowed major chord and a plucked folk tune. Every tune, chord order and rhythm passes `tests/unit/compose-library.test.mjs`.
  8. **No borrowed names.** "Scroll", "boon", "elite", "gate", "flask", "run": the genre's things, in plain words. No "cell", "mutation", "brutality", "tactics", "survival", "biome".

How to read a sprite row. **Meta:** type, role, view, themes, gameTypes, words, batch. **Frame:** size and pivot. **Body:** which body, and what it starts from. **Clips:** name, frame count, "loop" if it loops, then the label in brackets where the everyday word differs; fps and what a standard clip must show are in the style guide, section 4, and what is special is written here. **Parts:** each part with its selector's options, its other settings and its socket. **Controls:** the sprite's own controls by key, in the order of the file, each with kind, label, ends or options, default, what it is bound to, and its `when`. **Sounds:** clip, frame, sound id and style (the cue's `preset`), volume. **Versions:** name and `params`. **Uses:** every piece and generator the file names, with its version. **Drawing:** the detail layer. **Look and motion:** what a maker draws and a reviewer checks.

**Text and frames.** The kit's menus are the reused wooden set (`ui-buttons-wood`, `ui-panel-wood`) and the reused `ui-item-slots` and `ui-bars-rpg`, whose faces, frames and ink were measured when they were made (4.5 to 1 for white text on a face, 3 to 1 for a frame against its face, under every whole-bundle look). The one new interface sprite, `ui-hud-keep`, and the new icon set, `icons-boons`, hold no text area: they are glyphs and pictures, every clip `"noText": true` or a one-frame icon, so neither has a face that must carry white text. Their marks keep the 3 to 1 a frame needs against what they lie on: each row gives the pair, and the maker runs `worstContrast` of `engine/ui.js` under every whole-bundle look and reports the figure.

**Nothing is added to the palette.** A row that needs a pale wood uses `cream`; a dark of a doorway is `cavern`; rust is `ember`. Seven engine colours that are not palette names appear, each from an engine list the palette's groups already spread in, and each only as a swatch of a colour control, never as a fixed colour: on `keep-runner` the hair colours `#5a3a22`, `#a8612a`, `#e0b040` and `#d8d8e0` (`HAIR`), the bronze `#c98a4a` (`METAL`) and the whole engine skin list, as `knight`'s is; on `keep-champion` the two skin tones `#e8b088` and `#c98a5c` (`SKIN`) and nothing else (its bare head's hair is fixed thornwood.soil). Every fixed colour in the kit is a palette name.

## Reused assets

Thirty-six sprites, none changed (`reusedChanges` is empty). Each id is a file in `sprites/` today. The settings are the ones that sit best in this kit, chosen from each sprite's own options and swatches; the kit page may list them as starting versions.

### Props, a pickup, a projectile, glyphs and icons (pictures bodies)

All six: `"body": { "pictures": { "nodes": [...], "draw": [...], "per": {...}, "motion": [...], "emitters": [...] } }` (`docs/data-sprites.md` 2.5d). Shapes sit on nodes; clip keys move nodes; `per` gives a clip its own draw list; `frameEdits` show and hide shapes by frame. **`puffs@1`, `shards@1`, `sparkles@1`, `twinkle@2`, `rays@1`, `motes@1`, `spark.ring@1`, `pickup.burst@1`, `starburst@1` and `particles@1` are emitters only** (`context.mjs generators` tags each "name it as an emitter, not in a draw list on a node"): each is an entry of `emitters` with `"on": "<node>"`, turned on by a clip's keys (`"emit": { "<id>": { "amount": 1 } }`); a sprite that names one in a `draw` list fails validation. **Every emitter carries a `"clip": [...]` list** naming the clips it is keyed in, as each row's Uses line gives it, and an emitter that a toggle or a style turns off carries that `when` too. Where a row says "off elsewhere", the `clip` list is what does it: `boundEmitters` in `catalog/sprite-schema.js` adds up the stamp bound of every emitter whose `clip` and `when` let it draw in a clip ("amounts are not read"), so a key that sets `amount` to 0 does not take an emitter out of the 8,192 stamp pixels a frame may hold. **If the validator still reports "can paint up to N stamp pixels" for a clip**, the maker lowers the largest emitter's size table first and reports it, and never drops an emitter the row asks for. `gleam@1`, `glint@2` and `mark@1` go in draw lists. Light (`"glow": true`) is drawn after the `outline` and rimmed in its own `dark` tone. Clips are listed as one walk through the prop's life (style guide, section 4). The worked examples nearest to these are `sprites/wooden-door.json`, `sprites/upgrade-pedestal.json`, `sprites/scroll.json`, `sprites/arrow.json`, `sprites/ui-hud-platformer.json` and `sprites/icons-gear.json`.

All three: `"body": { "nodes": [...], "draw": [...], "emitters": [ ... ] }`: `body.emitters` is the array of emitters itself, and `nodes` and `draw` sit beside it in `body`, not inside it (`docs/data-sprites.md` 2.5c; `sprites/fx-heal.json` has exactly these three body keys). Each row's Uses line gives the `clip` list and the `when` of every emitter: copy them exactly. The draw list holds only shapes and `mark@1`. **The stamp budget:** `boundEmitters` in `catalog/sprite-schema.js` adds up the stamp bound of every emitter whose `clip` and `when` let it draw in a clip, for each clip and each setting the `when`s name, and does not read amounts, so a key that sets `amount` to 0 does not keep an emitter out of the 8,192 stamp pixels a frame may hold; only the `clip` list and the `when` do. `scroll-surge` `burst` (a glow, two rings, the swirl at radius 26, the lifting beam, with the beam or the leaves by style) and `elite-aura` `burst` (a floor ring 48 px wide, 20 px spikes, a ring, a glow, motes, and three flames in one style) are near the cap. **If the validator still reports "can paint up to N stamp pixels" for a clip**, the maker lowers the largest emitter's size table first (`swirl@1`'s radius, `starburst@1`'s `r`, `floor.ring@1`'s reach), reports the table before and after, and never drops an emitter the row asks for. Every particle of a looping clip has its own life phase so the loop closes. Every frame of every clip, at every setting, draws at least two pixels, and the last frame of every one-shot clip keeps three. **Start from `sprites/fx-heal.json`**: its `ring@1`, `beam.lift@1`, `blast.grow@1` and `motes@1` are separate behaviour emitters, each with a `clip` list (`["burst"]` or `["loop"]`) and one with a `when`, beside `nodes` and a `draw` list, turned on by `emit` keys. `sprites/dust-puff.json` shows a list of emitters a clip (`burst`, `kick`, `slide`) with `when` thresholds, and `sprites/micro-rocket.json` emitters with `clip` and `when` placed `"on"` nodes. `sprites/charge-up.json` (a `loop` and a `burst` in one sprite), `sprites/spawn-warp.json` (a ring on the floor) and `sprites/teleport-beam.json` (a column standing on the pivot row) are **look references only**: each is one opaque `effect.<id>@1` port a clip and shows no behaviour emitters, no draw list and no keys to copy.

## Left out, and why

- **A tucked roll and a wall climb as clips.** The side-view person base has no tucked pose and no climbing pose, and its limbs are baked for the poses it has. The dodge is a low dash; a game holds `jump` frame 4 against a wall. The first base that carries a roll should give this hero one.
- **A shield and a parry for the hero.** The reference game has both. The base has `block`, but a third thing in the runner's hands (a sword, a bow and a shield) does not read at 35 px. `guard-flash` (version "Parry") and `metal-hit` are in the library for a game that turns a well-timed `dodge` into a parry.
- **New bosses.** The row has two and the library has two for this world, `moss-golem` and `ember-wyrm`. A boss of the keep's own (a warden with several phases) belongs to the plan's wave 3, which adds large bosses with phases.
- **A new tileset and a new backdrop.** A prison, a sewer or a rampart is a new place, and the plan gives each world its tilesets once per view. `tiles-castle` has cells' worth of walls, windows and archways; `tiles-cave` is the way under. A rampart backdrop is the first thing a third side-view Thornwood bundle should add.
- **A common enemy that throws bombs, a shielded one, a flyer that dives.** `bone-soldier` has the shield and `bat` dives; `fused-bomb` is in the library, drawn top-down. Eight enemy kinds with two bosses is the row; this kit has seven and makes the seventh the one the library lacks, an archer.
- **More projectiles (the row has six).** A blade does most of this game's work. `arrow`, `throwing-knife` and `fireball` cover what flies; `boulder-chunk` is in the library for the golem.
- **Traps beyond spikes.** `saw-blade`, `crumble-block`, `moving-platform`, `spring-pad` and `lever` are in the library and suit the keep; the kit lists the one trap every area uses.
- **A key and a locked door between areas.** The gate is free or sealed. `gold-key` and `wooden-door` `locked` are in the library for side rooms.
- **A timed door, a challenge door, a cursed chest.** Each is one famous game's furniture (originality rule 4). `hourglass` and `ui-timer` are in the library for a game that wants a clock.
- **A currency that is carried out of the run and a collector to spend it with.** Originality rule 2. `gem` is the rarer coin; what a game keeps between runs is its own rule.
- **A minimap.** `ui-dungeon-map` (rooms, doors, marks) is in the library and works for a side-view area as it stands.
- **Damage numbers, status marks, a font, a name plate for an elite.** `ui-damage-digits`, `ui-status-icons`, `font-serif` and `ui-name-plate` are in the core library; the kit lists the five menu pieces every screen uses.
- **A weapon rack and a forge.** The room between areas has a fire, a keeper and a scroll stand; weapons come from chests and elites. A shop counter is `market-stall` (drawn top-down) or the keeper's `talk`.
- **A second hero body for a second class.** `rogue-battler` is the second figure. Classes are a game's rule, made from `weapon` and `bow`.
- **Portraits and talk scenes.** `ui-speech-bubble` and `emote-marks` are in the core library for the keeper's lines.
- **A roguelike theme or a side-view-roguelike game type in the catalogue.** Schema changes, outside a kit (plan, section 9).
- **The flat drawing style.** Decision P5 (the plan's section 5). Each row names its detail layer so it can follow.

## Batch plan

A batch starts when everything in its "Waits for" cell has passed review. Sounds first (a sprite cannot cue a sound that does not exist), then sprites, with music alongside. No batch adds a generator or a piece, and there is no helper batch. A sprite batch is 3 to 6 sprites of one body kind; no two batches write the same file; a sound batch is at most 6; a music batch is one track. Every reused asset is in `sprites/` or `sounds/` today: nothing waits on them.
