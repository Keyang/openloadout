# Core sounds, part 3: specification

Written 2026-11-19 for the asset plan (`docs/asset-plan.md`, section 2: core libraries, the lines "Menu sound sets in 5 house styles, 8 sounds each", "Weather and place loops", "General hits, whooshes, pickups, alerts and short reward tunes" and "Menu and credits themes, two in each of 12 music styles"). These assets belong to no single kit: any bundle may use them. 34 sounds and 6 music tracks in 13 batches (T01 to T13): the fifth menu sound set (8), ten more place loops, sixteen general sounds, and the credits themes of the six music styles that have none yet.

New sounds follow `.claude/skills/sprite-forge/SOUND.md` and `docs/style-guide.md` sections 11, 12 and 13. Where this page and the style guide disagree, this page wins; where this page is silent, the style guide and the skill decide. The format and the general rules of `bundles/specs/core-sounds-1.md` ("What is different about core sounds") and `bundles/specs/core-sounds-2.md` ("Rules for every sound on this page", "What a set is", the rules for all loops) apply here too, except where this page says otherwise.

## Rules for every asset on this page

These repeat the lessons of parts 1 and 2. A builder who has not read those pages reads this list first.

- **Do not duplicate** an existing sound in `sounds/` (165 files when this was written; `ls sounds/` first). Each row names **one** nearest existing sound; read that file first. Yours must be clearly different from it in what it is, not only in settings, and the row says how. No id on this page is used in `sounds/` or `sprites/` (checked when written; several obvious names are sprites already: `snowfall`, `rainfall`, `dust-storm`, `wind-gust`, `charge-up`, `campfire`, `trophy`).
- **Controls are exact.** The row's control list is the patch's `params`: ids, labels, ranges and defaults as written, no more and no fewer. Each control is written as `id` (Label: lowest to highest; default). Narrow a range only if an end is unusable, and say so in the report. `bright` is always Brightness, `pitch` is always Pitch (Hz, `"scale": "log"`), `length` is always Length (s, `"scale": "log"`), `speed` is always Speed (notes per second unless the row says otherwise), `space` is always Room.
- **Styles are exact.** Names, capitals and all, because sprites cue them by name. A style may only set controls the row declares.
- **Every way styles differ has a control.** Where styles differ in the kind of tone (a material, an instrument, a surface) the row gives a choice control with `options`; where they differ in shape (up or down, how many notes, how many strikes, whether a layer is there) the row gives a choice or stepped control. Never fake a different kind of tone with Brightness alone, and never describe a style its controls cannot reach. The three "kit styles" of part 1 ("Chiptune", "Storybook", "Gritty") are a difference in the kind of tone, so on this page every sound that has them also has the control `voice` (Instrument: options "Soft bell", "Blip", "Buzzy"; default as the row says): "Soft bell" is a soft pure tone with a small struck bell on it and a little room; "Blip" is a hollow, dry, toy-like tone (square, pulse or triangle); "Buzzy" is a rough, dry, crushed tone (saw or FM through `crush`). "Storybook" sets Soft bell, "Chiptune" sets Blip, "Gritty" sets Buzzy.
- **Roles fit.** Menu sounds sit under `interface/`. Loops are `environment/weather` (weather) or `environment/ambience` (a place), type `ambience`, and every loop here loops. A one-shot never takes an `environment/` role. A short tune of 1 to 3 s is a jingle (`"category": "jingle"`, `meta.type: 'jingle'`, never `music`), whatever its role.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13): no sound or tune may be close enough to a famous one that a player would name the source. No tune of two notes a fourth apart on a hollow square tone, no rising major arpeggio ending on a held top note, no four-note rising "secret" figure, no victory tune of quick repeated pick-up notes rising to held brass chords, no ending of flat-six, flat-seven, home. Each row with a tune gives it as steps (semitones) up or down from the home note; build exactly those steps and repeat them in the report.
- **Repeats vary, interface does not.** Hits, swings, drops, breaks, scatters and loops differ from take to take (`rnd()`, `noise`, `dust`: the checker's variation line above 0.2 dB). Menu sounds, pickups with a tune, alerts and jingles are identical every take (variation 0.0). Each section says which.
- **Framework:** in this run, do **not** edit `sound/`, `quality/`, `tools/` (except, for a music track, your own new `tools/compose/<id_with_underscores>.mjs`) or any file other than your own `sounds/<id>.json`. Several builders work at once. If the engine lacks something you need, make the nearest sound you can and say in the report what was missing. You therefore skip the fingerprint step of SOUND.md.
- **Scratch files.** Builders share one scratch folder, `out/`. Name every scratch file with your sound id (`out/<id>.png`, `out/<id>-<style>.png`, `out/<id>-try2.json`). Never write a draft into `sounds/` (the build picks up every file there), and never delete or overwrite a file you did not make.
- **Phone speakers:** at least 40% of the energy above 300 Hz at the default and in every style; measure it with the snippet in SOUND.md. The rows at risk say which layer carries the mid range.
- **Words a visitor reads** (name, description, labels, options, style names) are everyday words: what is heard, not how it is made (SOUND.md lists the banned words). The "Built from" text in the rows below is for the builder and uses the engine's words.

## Menu sound set 5: harp (T01, T02)

The fifth set of eight, after wood, glass, warm console and soft bubble. Everything in "What a set is" of `core-sounds-2.md` holds: the same eight id endings, roles and style names, so a game switches its whole menu voice by changing only the id prefix (`menu-glass-confirm` to `menu-harp-confirm`, style "Accept" in both).

Notes for the set:

- Style values differ the same way as in the other four sets: "Grid" and "Pop-up" a little higher and shorter (and "Pop-up" sets `strings` 3), "Tab", "Inventory" and "Start game" longer (and "Inventory" sets `strings` 7), "Wrong answer" lower and longer than "Not allowed", "Cancel" lower than "Back". "Switch on" and "Switch off" set `dir`; "Buy" sets `notes` 3 and "Equip" 2.
- Where `speed` sets the length it is the sound's "how long" and there is no `length`. `speed`'s range is chosen so the length stays inside the row's range; if it does not, narrow `speed` and say so.
- `dir` is a choice control. The checker may warn that it changes the level by little: an up-or-down flip is a change of shape, not loudness. Compare renders of both ends and say so in the report.
- Phone speakers: the home note is 330 Hz and the soundbox adds energy at 230 Hz. Measure every style, above all the low ones ("Cancel", "Wrong answer", "Start game"); if one falls under 40% above 300 Hz, lower `body` in that style rather than raising Brightness.
- The `pluck` node takes its pitch as a signal, so row 7's step inside one pluck needs no second node. If the step clicks, say so and use the nearest clean form (a very soft second pluck).

## Place loops (T02 to T04)

**All loops:** type `ambience`, category `ambience`, `"loop": true`, duration as the row says, stereo where it helps, varies from take to take (`rnd()`, `noise`, `dust`). No single event so distinctive that the repeat is heard: an event that stands out (a thunder roll, a knock, a clink, a console beep) is varied in pitch and timing from take to take and happens at most a few times per loop; the row gives the count. Any regular motion completes whole cycles in the loop.

**Seam** (the lesson of part 2). `"wrap": true` crossfades the seam over only 8 ms, so any slow random motion (`clock` to `rand` to `lag` swells and gates) jumps in level at the seam on every repeat. Only two loops here set `"wrap": true`: row 17 `ship-bridge` (a steady tone, which the default 0.3 s crossfade would make swell or dip at every repeat) and row 18 `haunted-hall` (long reverb tails that must cross the seam). In those two, every tonal layer is either filtered noise or a tone held to whole cycles in the loop at every setting (a multiple of 0.125 Hz in 8 s: round the frequency), and every slow swell or glide is built from periodic motion that completes whole cycles in the loop, never from slow random motion. All other loops leave wrap off, get the default 0.3 s crossfade and may use slow random motion.

**Loudness.** `"normalize": false` for a loop whose loudness follows an amount control, and for those only: rows 9 and 10 (`amount`) and row 11 (`rain` and `thunder`, which are the levels of its two layers), with a raw peak of 0.4 to 0.7 at the loudest setting and at most 0.95 everywhere. Every other loop, row 15 included, is normalised as usual: row 15 has no amount control (a bigger or cheering crowd is a different sound, not a louder one), and how loud it plays is the cue's volume. No volume control.

**Meta.** `meta.themes`: the place first when the theme list has it (`forest`, `dungeon`, `city`, `sci-fi`, `space`, `horror`), otherwise `neutral` first; then up to two more. `meta.gameTypes`: the kind of game that most needs the place first, then up to four more. Loops have no `length` (the loop length is the length); "how high" is `size` or `pitch` as the row says.

**Not these.** No plain wind (`wind`), no fire or hearth (`fire`), no rain alone (`rain`, `rain-roof`), no leaves in a breeze (`forest-rustle`), no cave, sea, stream, city, machine room, cricket night or underwater (all exist). No birds, frogs, dogs, horses, words or laughter: synthesis is poor at animals and speech. Rows 15 and 16 ask for a crowd; read their warning.

## Core music: six credits themes (T08 to T13)

The library has a menu theme for all twelve music styles (`menu-<band>`) and a credits theme for six (`credits-musicbox`, `credits-organ`, `credits-steelpan`, `credits-lofi`, `credits-twang`, `credits-darkambient`). These six finish the pairs, which brings core music to 24 tracks.

Each is a patch with `"category": "music"` and `meta.type: 'music'`, written by a generator `tools/compose/credits_<band>.mjs` on the music kit with `band('<band>')` (`docs/music-styles.md`, section 4; `sound/bands.js`). Follow the Music section of SOUND.md and the style guide, section 12. **Read the band's own menu track first** (`tools/compose/menu_<band>.mjs`: how the band's voices are called and mixed) and one finished credits track (`tools/compose/credits_steelpan.mjs`: the credits layer plan and its 32-bar build). Yours must share no chord order, no tune and at most one bar of tune rhythm with any track in the library: `node --test tests/unit/compose-library.test.mjs` says so and must pass.

For all six:

- **Role:** `music/victory`, settled: the same role as the six existing credits tracks (`credits-musicbox`, `credits-organ`, `credits-steelpan`, `credits-lofi`, `credits-twang`, `credits-darkambient`), so all twelve credits themes share one role and no existing file needs a change. The role tree has no credits role; `music/menu` stays for the twelve `menu-<band>` tracks only. Nothing waits on a decision: T08 to T13 can start at once.
- **Controls, exactly** (style guide section 12): `intensity` (Intensity: 0 to 1; 0.85), `bpm` (Speed: the row's range; the row's default), `key` (Pitch: -5 to 6 semitones; 0), `bars` (Length: 8 to 32, `"step": 8`; **32**), `inst` (Melody instrument: the five standard options in order; the row's default), `bright` (Brightness: 0 to 1; the band's default), `space` (Room: 0 to 0.5; the band's default), `lead` (Melody: 0 to 1; 0.8), and `swing` (Bounce: 0 to 1; the row's default) only where the row lists it. Eight or nine controls.
- **Tempo** lies inside the band's range (`B.checkTempo`), and its top plus 5% still does. 32 bars at the slowest tempo last under 120 s.
- **Layers and intensity**, the same plan as the existing credits tracks so a game can swap one for another: (1) the bed, there at Intensity 0 and still music; (2) the chord voice, in by 0.22; (3) the tune, in by 0.4, so it is playing at 0.5 as the whole-bundle looks need; (4) the drums, in by 0.6; (5) the band's own lead answering the tune, in by 0.85. Each row names what its five are.
- **Length:** 8, 16, 24 or 32 bars. The 32-bar version builds section by section: bed and chords, then the tune, then the drums, then the answers; the 8-bar version is the full band; a longer version is never the short one repeated.
- **Three styles**, named for moments, exactly as the row writes them. The first is the default setting.
- **Chords** are the row's, one per bar for 8 bars, and the checker must find them. Each order was compared bar by bar with all 24 tracks in `sounds/` and with the other five on this page (counted from the first bar's root, as the test counts; re-run after rows 38 and 39 were changed). What that check found, no more and no less: no whole order matches; no track has the same first half in bars 1 to 4 or the same second half in bars 5 to 8 as any of these six; the most bars any pair shares in place is 5 of 8 (row 35 with `menu-folk`, row 40 with `menu-orchestra`), 4 for rows 36 and 37, and 3 for rows 38 and 39. Two halves do appear in another track at the other end of its loop: row 37's bars 5 to 8 (0M 9m 2m 7M) are bars 1 to 4 of `menu-marimba`, and row 38's bars 1 to 4 (0M 5M 7M 0M) are bars 5 to 8 of `hearthvale-village`. Most tracks in a major key start on the home chord and end on the fifth, so a claim that two tracks share nothing in place is nearly always false: an Originality line says exactly which bars agree.
- **Tune:** the row gives its shape and its rhythm; the builder writes the notes. A phrase of 2 or 4 bars that returns, a range of at most two octaves inside all five instruments' ranges. Say in the report what the tune is, bar by bar, and paste the library test's result.
- **Meta:** `themes`: `neutral`. `gameTypes`: three that suit the band, as its menu track has. `words`: 6 to 14, with `credits`, `ending` and the band's own words (`B.words`), never a word from the banned list of `docs/music-styles.md` section 8 ("real", "acoustic", "orchestral", "live", "authentic", any brand).
- **Description:** says "synthesised" and names what the layers are in everyday words, as `credits-steelpan`'s does.
- **Tests:** add the track's row to `PINNED` and its name to the generator list in `tests/unit/compose-tracks.test.mjs` in the same change (the only test edit allowed), as every new track does.
