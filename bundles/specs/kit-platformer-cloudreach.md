# Cloudreach precision platformer kit: specification (`kit-platformer-cloudreach`)

Written 2026-11-22 by the art director for the asset plan (`docs/asset-plan.md` in `lab/sprite-forge/`, section 7, wave 1, row 16: "Cloudreach: precision platformer", side view). It is the third bundle of wave 1, after Gridline (`kit-puzzle-gridline.md`) and Hearthvale (`kit-adventure-hearthvale.md`). It goes with `kit-platformer-cloudreach.plan.json`; the two always agree. **Nothing new in it is built yet; the 30 reused sprites, 24 reused sounds and 3 reused tracks are in `sprites/` and `sounds/` today.** **Read `docs/style-guide.md` first** (clip names, frame counts, cue frames, control keys and labels, metadata, originality), then `docs/data-sprites.md` section 2 (the data format) and the skill (`../../skills/sprite-forge/SKILL.md`, `SOUND.md`). This file says what each asset is, and where it departs from the guide it says so and why. Where this page and the style guide disagree, this page wins for that asset; where this page is silent, the style guide and the skill decide.

| | |
|---|---|

## What is different about this kit

- **What the checklist asks for, and nothing of the look.** The reference game (plan, section 1) gives the list: an air dash and its refill, spikes, moving and falling blocks, wind, a collectible in every room, room checkpoints; and around them what any side-view platformer needs: a hero, enemies and a boss, ground and platforms, pickups, effects, backdrops, menus, sounds and five tracks. Everything here is drawn and named for Cloudreach. The originality rules for this kit are at the end of this list.
- **Reuse first: 30 of the 46 sprites, 24 of the 32 sounds and 3 of the 5 tracks exist already.** The plan's row counts 30 new sprites for a platformer; this kit makes 16. Spikes, moving platforms, crumbling cloud blocks, springs, the checkpoint flag, doors and keys, five enemies, the boss, every effect and the menus are in the library. New is only what the world needs once (three tilesets, three backdrops, its hero and two creatures) and what the reference game adds that nothing in the library does (a dash refill with a spent state, a block that falls, things that show and make wind, the place a letter is delivered, dash pips for the screen, and stamps for the level cards).
- **Every sprite is data, and no generator, piece or drawing code is added.** Each new sprite is one file `sprites/<id>.json` (`"format": "loadout-sprite", "version": 2`) with one body: three `rig`, three `tiles`, three `layers` and seven `pictures`. Each row names the base, the pieces and the generators it uses; **every one was checked to exist on 2026-11-22** with `node ../../skills/sprite-forge/context.mjs generators` and `... pieces` (307 generators, 119 pieces). Where nothing fits (both creatures, the props), the sprite is drawn from the format's own shapes (`ellipse`, `capsule`, `limb`, `poly`, `rect`, `line`, `arc`, `ring`, `stamp`: `docs/data-sprites.md` 2.7). A maker who finds something missing stops that sprite and reports "blocked: <what is missing>".
- **The palette is new and needs one engine step this specification does not take.** The colours are in the plan file. `node tools/bundles-from-plans.mjs --check --root .` therefore says "engine/palettes.js does not have it yet" for this kit until batch CK0 adds them ([New framework](#new-framework)). A data sprite writes hex values, so no sprite batch waits for CK0; only the kit's bundle file does.
- **The kit's size is its own.** 32 sounds and 5 tracks are above what a kit holds without saying so (`SMALL_KIT_SIZE`: 8 to 30 sounds, 1 to 4 tracks). The plan carries `"size": { "sprites": [42, 50], "sounds": [28, 34], "music": [4, 5] }`, which `kitSize` in `tools/lib/assets.mjs` reads today.
- **Themes:** `fantasy` first, then at most one of `forest` (island things) or `dungeon` (temple things). Interface: `neutral`, `fantasy`. `catalog/schema.js` has no sky theme; adding one is outside a kit.
- **gameTypes:** `platformer` first on everything; `arcade` on pickups, interface and icons; `roguelike-action` on hazards and enemies.
- **The player is `cloud-courier`, new.** It is on the side-view person base, and the air dash is its `dodge` clip (label "Air dash"): the playground plays a clip called `dodge` on its dash key and moves the character while it plays.
- **Deviations from the style guide, each with its reason; makers say so in the report:**
  - `cloud-courier` has no `attack`, `shoot` or `cast` (section 4: every player has one): a courier in a precision platformer strikes nothing. Its action is the dash. It has no held part (section 9: a character has one held part and one worn): its hands are free for the dash, and the thing it carries is its `pack`, a part on the `back` socket.
  - `cloud-courier`'s `dodge` is an air dash, not a roll: frame 1 is a gathering crouch in the air and frames 2 to 5 are one held stretched pose, with no tuck.
  - `gust-toad`'s `attack` is a blown gust with no reach (label "Blow"): the body leans back, not forward, on the hit frame.
  - `gale-gull` has no `attack` (section 4: every enemy has one): it is a thing to avoid and to bounce on, and it harms only by being flown into. It has `fly`, a `dive` (the moment it drops when stood on), `hurt` and `die`. Its frame is 64x48 with pivot [32, 24] and `grounded: false`, which is no row of section 1: a flyer 31 to 41 px long, 50 px with its longest tail, does not fit the flyer's 48x48 with a border, so it takes the long creature's 64x48, and its pivot is the frame's centre, as every flyer's is, not the long creature's [28, 43], which is a standing point.
  - `tiles-cloudbank` has no `wear` (section 10: always, for a tileset): nothing wears on a cloud. Its `wisp` platform is drawn without an ink line on its underside, which fades into three loose pixels: it is a platform a player drops through.
  - `falling-slab` has no `size` control (section 10): `width` and `thickness` instead, because a game fits it to a gap. (Its frame, 64x48 with pivot [32, 46], is the guide's "Prop, medium": no deviation.)
  - `wind-sock` has no `hurt` or `break`; its three clips are a walk through calm, filling and full, as `checkpoint-banner`'s are.
  - `gust-pinwheel` is a pickup with four clips (section 4 gives a pickup two): `spent` and `respawn` are the reference game's addition, a refill that comes back.
  - `ui-hud-courier` is a glyph set of 12 clips (a set has 6 to 10), as `ui-hud-platformer` (11) and `ui-dungeon-map` (13) are. It has no `size` control (section 10: an icon or glyph set always has `shine` and `size`), as `ui-hud-platformer` has none: a HUD glyph is drawn at one size, 14x14 in its 16x16 cell, so that a row of pips lines up; a game scales the whole HUD.
  - `bg-wind-temple` runs dark to light from far to near (section 7 allows it for a dark place): a storm sky behind pale stone. Its standard `time` control (section 10: day, dusk, night, label "Time") is labelled "Weather" with the options storm | clearing | night: the place is always under a storm, so what changes is the weather, and the three options map onto `sky.look`'s dusk, day and night.
- **Meaning stays fixed** (style guide section 3, rule 7): `crimson` is danger (spike tips, the storm's warning lights). **A danger red that no control paints is a fixed slot with `"material": "warning"`**, never a bare fixed colour: a fixed slot otherwise takes the sprite's rest material, `paint`, which a whole-bundle look may repaint (style guide section 10, "Whole-bundle looks"). The three are the warning mark of `falling-slab`, the warning lamp of `gust-vent` and the seal of `ui-hud-courier`'s `letter`. A fixed light takes `"material": "light"` the same way (the lantern of `wind-sock`); `gold` is reward (stamps earned, the delivered flag); `gust` (the shared friendly cyan `#4fd8ee`) is the courier's wind: a ready dash, the pinwheel, lit goggles; `hero` blue is the courier's own cloth and **no enemy offers `hero` as a swatch**; `squall` purple is the enemy accent.
- **Colours are written as palette names.** `cloudreach.chalk` in this page is the hex of `chalk` in the table below; in the file a slot's `default` and its `swatches` are those hex values, in the order given. A swatch list written "exactly" is the whole list. A colour that is not a control is a palette colour by name in a slot with no control, or a `derive` of another slot; never a typed shadow. A slot's `material` is one of the engine's sixteen (`MATERIAL_IDS` in `engine/materials.js`); where a row gives none, the label decides it as the engine does.
- **Touching colour controls never share a swatch, every list holds 4 to 8, and two materials that touch differ in brightness by 0.12 or more at their defaults.** Each row says which lists touch and gives the figures. One exception, as in Hearthvale: a light colour (`glow`: drawn after the outline with its own dark rim) may share one swatch with a list it touches if no version sets the two equal. Makers check Sunset, Night, Handheld and Mono (`tools/render.mjs`, `tools/scene.mjs`).
- **Every slider states its default.** A slider is `"kind": "range"` running 0 to 1 with default 0.5 unless the row gives `min`, `max`, `step` and a default; a part's size or length is a multiplier 0.65 to 1.35, step 0.05, default 1. A slider does nothing by itself in a data sprite: the row says what it is bound to (a `scales` list, a binding table on a shape or a generator argument, a clip's `travel`). `speed` is always `{ "key": "speed", "standard": "speed" }`. Controls the row marks **Motion** carry `"group": "Motion"`; a part's settings are listed in the part; no other control has a group.
- **`when`.** A control or a cue followed by **when** `{ ... }` is in effect only for those values; copy it exactly. A toggle written "in every style" has no `when`.
- **Ready-made versions.** Every row lists 4 or 5 `versions`; the first is always the defaults (`params: {}`). A name is at most four everyday words, repeats no word and is not a control's label. Each must pass the checker and look different at card size on the preview clip from every other. If one does not, change its settings, keep its name and say so in the report.
- **Sounds.** A new sprite cues only sounds in this kit's list of 32: the 24 reused (in `sounds/` today, with the styles named) or the 8 new (a sprite batch waits for the sound batch it cues). `idle` is silent except where a row gives a looping sound on a looping clip. Tiles, backdrops, glyphs and icons carry no cues. A clip of an interface sprite that carries no text says `"noText": true`; the two interface rows say which.
- **Drawing styles (Contract 12).** Decision P5 holds: no row asks for the flat style. Every sprite is `"styles": ["pixel"]`, draws bodies with shapes and keeps single pixels and stamps for the detail layer the row names.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13 and the plan's section 1). Rules for this kit, on top of the guide's:
  1. **The hero is a courier, and reads as one.** A short figure in blue with a canvas mailbag, goggles pushed up on a leather cap, and a short scarf. No long red hair, no hair or scarf that changes colour when the dash is spent (the dash is shown by `ui-hud-courier` and by the goggles' lens, which is the same colour always), no parka, no climbing gear. No version offers that look.
  2. **The refill is a pinwheel.** Not a floating green diamond crystal, not a feather. The two-dash pickup is the library's `power-orb`.
  3. **The room collectible is a letter** (`scroll`, shape "letter"). No strawberry, no fruit with wings, nothing that flies off when the player dashes.
  4. **No famous level furniture.** No block with a face that slams when dashed beside, no traffic-light block on a track, no bubble that carries the player, no jellyfish to hold as a glider, no puffer fish that bursts, no pink cloud. The moving block is the library's platform, the falling one is a slab of temple stone, the bouncing cloud is the library's crumbling block and spring.
  5. **No mountain.** The world is sky: no summit flag, no cable car, no ruined hotel, no mirror double of the hero as a boss. The boss is the library's wyrm in storm colours.
  6. **No borrowed music.** No solo piano over synth pads as the level theme; the tunes are on strings, flute and organ pipes. Every tune, chord order and rhythm passes `tests/unit/compose-library.test.mjs`.
  7. **No borrowed names.** "Air dash", "refill", "checkpoint", "falling block", "wind": the genre's things, in plain words.

How to read a sprite row. **Meta:** type, role, view, themes, gameTypes, words, batch. **Frame:** size and pivot. **Body:** which body, and what it starts from. **Clips:** name, frame count, "loop" if it loops, then the label in brackets where the everyday word differs; fps and what a standard clip must show are in the style guide, section 4, and what is special is written here. **Parts:** each part with its selector's options, its other settings and its socket. **Controls:** the sprite's own controls by key, in the order of the file, each with kind, label, ends or options, default, what it is bound to, and its `when`. **Sounds:** clip, frame, sound id and style (the cue's `preset`), volume. **Versions:** name and `params`. **Uses:** every piece and generator the file names, with its version. **Drawing:** the detail layer. **Look and motion:** what a maker draws and a reviewer checks.

## The Cloudreach palette

Open sky, sunlit cloud and pale stone, with warm brass, teak and canvas for what people built. **The world is light and the things in it are dark or saturated**: the sky, the clouds and the rock are all brighter than 0.7, so everything a player must see (the hero, enemies, hazards, pickups) is darker than 0.65 or carries an ink line against it.

**Which colours touch, and how they stay apart.** The pairs this kit's defaults put side by side, each 0.12 or more apart: turf on chalk (0.59, 0.82); chalk on shale (0.82, 0.57); shale on slate (0.57, 0.38); cloud on mist (0.92, 0.80); mist on storm (0.80, 0.35); patina on chalk (0.56, 0.82); teak on sail (0.48, 0.85); brass on teak (0.63, 0.48); brass on hero (0.63, 0.42); sail on hero (0.85, 0.42); skin on hero (0.85, 0.42); crimson on steel (0.41, 0.79); gust on slate (0.70, 0.38); patina on slate (0.56, 0.38: the falling slab and its trim); squall on turf (0.41, 0.59); rose on fern (0.59, 0.44: the toad's sac on its hide). **Pairs that are too close and are never put side by side as two materials:** chalk, mist, dawn and sail (0.80 to 0.85); sky, gust and gold (0.70 to 0.75); hero, crimson, squall and fern (0.41 to 0.44); teak and copper (0.48); shale, patina, dusk, turf and rose (0.55 to 0.59). No swatch list of two controls that touch holds two colours from one of those groups, except where a row says an ink line or a dark rim lies between them.

**Text and frames.** The kit's menus are the reused paper set (`ui-buttons-paper`, `ui-panel-paper`), whose faces and ink were measured when they were made. The two new interface sprites hold no text area: `ui-hud-courier` is glyphs and `icons-postmarks` is pictures, so neither has a face that must carry white text. Their marks keep the 3 to 1 a frame needs against what they lie on: each row gives the pair (`worstContrast` of `engine/ui.js`, run by the maker under every whole-bundle look).

## Left out, and why

- **Wall climbing as a clip.** The reference game's third move is catching and climbing a wall. The side-view person base has no climbing pose, and its keys are baked for the poses it has: a hand-written climb would not hold the base's limbs together. A game holds `jump` frame 4 against a wall and plays `wall-grab`. The first base that carries a climb should add `climb` to this hero.
- **A new boss.** The row has one and the library has it: `ember-wyrm`, whose wing buffet is already a wall of wind. A storm-coloured boss of Cloudreach's own belongs to the world's second side-view bundle (the run-and-gun), which needs large bosses.
- **A seventh creature and a third projectile.** A precision platformer is about the room, not its enemies; six creatures is already more than the reference game leans on. Nothing here shoots but the boss and the imp.
- **A balloon lift and a rotor platform.** `moving-platform` with its chains off and its gear turning reads as a small rotor craft; a second moving block would be the same thing again.
- **A second, bigger refill and a feather for flight.** The two-dash refill is `power-orb`. Free flight is a famous power of the reference game and is not on the first-hour checklist.
- **Hearts and health.** One touch sends the courier back; `ui-hud-courier` counts falls instead. `heart` and `ui-hud-platformer` are in the library for a game that wants health.
- **Portraits and talk scenes.** `ui-speech-bubble` and `emote-marks` are in the core library for the wind-reader's lines; the reference game's portrait conversations are large pictures, outside what this kit needs.
- **Weather overlays and more effects.** `mist-drift`, `sun-rays`, `petal-fall` ("Seeds on the wind"), `rainfall` and `zap-crackle` are in the library and suit the levels; a game adds them as it likes. The kit lists the five effects every room uses.
- **An airship tileset.** Decks, ladders and posts are `tiles-platforms`. A hull with portholes and a sail is the first thing the world's next side-view bundle should add.
- **A sky theme and a precision-platformer game type in the catalogue.** Schema changes, outside a kit (plan, section 9).
- **The flat drawing style.** Decision P5 (the plan's section 5). Each row names its detail layer so it can follow.

## Batch plan

A batch starts when everything in its "Waits for" cell has passed review. Sounds first (a sprite cannot cue a sound that does not exist), then sprites, with music alongside. No batch adds a generator or a piece. A sprite batch is 3 to 6 sprites of one body kind; no two batches write the same file; a sound batch is at most 6; a music batch is one track. Every reused asset is in `sprites/` or `sounds/` today: nothing waits on them.
