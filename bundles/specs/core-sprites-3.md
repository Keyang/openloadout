## What is different in part 3

- **What part 3 covers.** Text: a pixel font in four styles drawn by a new helper, and the pieces that show words and numbers (font sheets, a score counter, a timer, score pops, word pops); the pieces that carry a game's own words (speech and thought bubbles, name plates); marks over a character's head (emotes); and three more menu skins. No row draws a character body, and no row declares parts.
- **Letters are drawn here, on purpose (a deviation from style guide section 8, which says "no words or letters drawn").** The font sheets, the word pops, the score pops, the sample clips of the score counter and the timer, and the sleep emote's Zs draw letters or digits: that is what they are for. Everything that **carries** a game's text still draws none in its text area: the bubbles, the name plate, the counter's `frame` clip, and every menu-kit piece. Builders say so in the report.
- **Frame sizes and pivots outside the style guide's table (deviations from sections 1 and 8).** Besides the 16x20 font cell (given under [The font sheets](#the-font-sheets)), these rows use a size or a pivot the table does not give, each for its reason; builders say so in the report:
  - `ui-score-counter` 96x24 and `ui-timer` 64x24 (role `interface/panel`; the table gives 96x64): a HUD strip one row of digits tall. 24 rows hold the large digit (cap 9) in its window, the case band and the ink with 1 px to spare; a 64 px tall plate would be two thirds empty and cover the play field. The timer is 64 wide because it holds at most 4 digits and a clock.
  - `ui-score-pop` 48x32 (role `interface/icon`; the table gives 32x32 or 16x16): `+1000` at the large chunky size, rimmed, is about 45 px wide, and the rise needs 8 rows above the number. Pivot [24, 24], not the centre: the bottom middle of the number at rest, the spot the points rise from, with 8 rows of room above it.
  - `ui-speech-bubble` and `ui-thought-bubble` 96x48 (role `interface/panel`; the table gives 96x64): a bubble holds one or two lines of speech over a character; at 96x64 it would hide the speaker's neighbours. 48 rows hold two lines of medium text, the band, a 5 px tail and the shadow. Both stretch, so a game makes them taller.
  - `ui-name-plate` 64x16 (role `interface/panel`, at the bar size): one line of small text over a head; 16 rows hold a medium cap (7) with the band, ink and shadow, and it stretches sideways to the name. Pivot [32, 15], not the centre: the bottom middle, the point above the head it sits on.
  - `ui-word-pop` 96x32 (role `interface/icon`, at the button size): a word is wider than an icon cell; `GAME OVER` at the large chunky size, rimmed, is about 70 px.
  - `emote-marks` and `emote-bubbles` pivot [16, 30], not the centre: the bottom middle, the point above the head the mark stands on (row 31 is kept for the shadow).
- **A text area holds only the face colour, in every style.** Every clip that declares a `textArea` has, inside that rectangle, nothing but the flat face colour, for every option of `style`, every toggle and every swatch: no spike, sparkle, leaf, icicle, gloss line, badge rim or digit reaches into it (the contrast test, `contrastFaults` of `engine/ui.js`, checks every colour inside a text area under every whole-bundle look). Each helper on this page returns the rectangle that stays flat after its marks; take the text area from that return value, never from the frame size.
- **Every id is new in both folders.** All 25 ids were checked on 2026-11-19 against every file in `sprites/` and `sounds/` and against every sound id planned in `core-sounds-1.md` and `core-sounds-2.md`: none is taken (the build refuses two assets with one id). A builder who finds one taken all the same stops and reports it; never rename on your own.
- **Every row of a button or a cursor sprite is a state and carries a label of 12 or more characters saying when it is shown.** This now includes `normal`, `hover`, `pressed` and `disabled` (part 2 labelled only the tabs): the download guide writes each state row from its label.
- **The new skins are drawn over the `flat` skin.** `engine/ui-skins.js` knows four skins and refuses any other name, and its exports must not change (additive rule). So each new skin (`neon`, `crystal`, `grove`) draws its slab with the `flat` skin of `engine/ui-skins.js` (`skinButton`, `skinPanel`, `skinCheck` and the rest, skin `'flat'`), re-cuts its corners with `roundSlab` of `engine/ui-candy-slab.js`, and adds its look with the new `engine/ui-skins-more.js` (H5), the way the candy, paper and gilded sprites of part 2 finish their pieces with `engine/ui-candy.js`, `engine/ui-paper.js` and `engine/ui-gilded.js`. The flat skin's rules carry over unchanged: white text, the face lightened at most `tone(face, 0.1)` on hover, the band brightened instead. Swatch lists pass through `skinSwatches('flat', ...)` of `engine/ui-skins.js`, whose rules (white text 4.5 to 1, a lighter frame 3 to 1 against every kept face, an accent that is no face or frame) are exactly the new skins' rules.
- **The palette rule is part 1's, unchanged**: defaults are Thornwood colours by name, with at most two colours of the other palettes appended to a list; lists of 4 to 8, all distinct, none darker than brightness 0.18; meaning stays fixed (danger red, healing green, friendly energy cyan, reward gold). A name with no palette is Thornwood (`gold` is `PALETTE.thornwood.gold`); `hearthvale.wheat` is `PALETTE.hearthvale.wheat`. **Touching colour controls never share a swatch**, and each row says which touch. The interface lists that carry text were measured with `worstContrast` of `engine/ui.js` on 2026-11-19; the lowest pair is given with each list.
- **Sound cues** go only to sounds in `sounds/` today: `menu-bubble-open` ("Pop-up"), `menu-bubble-close` ("Pop-up"), `menu-glass-confirm`, `menu-glass-move`, `menu-glass-toggle`, `menu-console-confirm`, `menu-console-move`, `menu-console-toggle`, `menu-wood-confirm`, `menu-wood-move`, `menu-wood-toggle` ("Select", "Tab", "Switch on"), `xp-blip` ("XP shard", "Tiny tick", "Combo high"), `timer-tick` ("Clock", "Bomb timer"), `countdown-go`, `level-up`, `quest-complete`, `game-over` ("Chiptune") and `notify-ping` ("Storybook"). No batch waits on a sound batch.
- **Ready-made versions.** Every row lists 3 to 6 `versions` (docs/site-design.md section 4). The first is always the defaults (`params: {}`). Each must pass the checker and **look different at card size on the preview clip** (the first clip unless the row names another) from the defaults and from every other version (`VERSION_RULES` in `engine/versions.js`). If one does not, change its settings, keep its name and say so in the report.
- **Originality** (style guide section 13 and the plan's section 1). Each row names the famous design it must not become, where there is one.

How to read a row: as in parts 1 and 2. **Clips:** name, frame count, "loop" if it loops, then the label in brackets. **Controls:** the sprite's own controls by key, in `params` order; **every slider states its default**; standard keys take the style guide's label and end words (section 10). **Count sliders** are whole-number ranges with step 1 (`range('Digits', 6, { min: 3, max: 7, step: 1, lo: 'Few', hi: 'Many' })`), and versions set them to whole numbers inside the range. A colour's note gives its label, its default and its swatches exactly. **when:** a control or cue followed by **when** `{ ... }` is in effect only for those values; copy it exactly. A toggle written "in every style" does something in every option of every choice. **Sounds:** clip, frame, sound id and style, volume. **Versions:** name and `params`. **Helpers:** every framework function the sprite calls, by module; **(new, H4)** or **(new, H5)** marks a function that does not exist yet and is built in that helper batch ([New helpers](#new-helpers)). Every function not so marked was checked to exist on 2026-11-19.

## New helpers

Every function below does not exist today (checked against every `export` in `engine/` on 2026-11-19). They are built **before** any sprite that uses them, in the helper batches H4 and H5, by the skill's rules for the framework (Contract 11): tests first, additions only (five new modules; no existing file is edited), a doc comment above each export, and the pixel fingerprint of every existing sprite identical before and after. A sprite builder who finds one short of an option adds that option to the same module the same way and lists it in the report; a sprite file never holds generic drawing code. Each module is one builder's at a time.

### H4a: `engine/pixel-font.js` (new module): a pixel font in four styles

Why it is needed: `engine/ui.js` draws digits only (`digitSize`, `digitBody`, `rimmed`), and `engine/glyphs.js` hand-sets tiny pictures; nothing draws a letter, a punctuation mark or a line of text, and nothing knows a baseline or spacing (part 2, "Left out").

**The idea.** Letters are built the way `digitBody` builds digits: strokes in box units (x 0 to 1 across the glyph, y on named lines: the cap line, the x-height, the middle, the baseline, the descender line), filled on a `glyphGrid` (`engine/ui.js`) by the style's rules. Diagonal strokes (A, K, M, N, V, W, X, Y, Z, k, v, w, x, y, z, /) are drawn as even pixel stairs of the stroke width. **Digits are `digitBody` of `engine/ui.js`**, passed the font's own size object (`{ w, h: cap, stroke, bar, round }`), so the font's digits are drawn by the same rule as `ui-damage-digits` (they match only where the sizes match: the chunky digit bodies are 5x5, 6x7 and 7x9 here and 6x7, 6x9 and 7x9 in `ui-damage-digits`); the serif style adds its feet to them. Where a computed glyph fails the tests at the small size, it is hand-set as rows (the form of `glyphPicture` in `engine/glyphs.js`: `#` body, `.` nothing) in the exported table `FONT_HAND`, and the tests treat it like any other glyph. No glyph is copied from an existing commercial or freely licensed pixel font: every one comes from these stroke tables or from `FONT_HAND`.

| Style | Strokes | Turns | Feet |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chunky` | uprights `stroke` 2 px, bars 1 px below cap 9, 2 px at 9 (the `digitSize` rule) | square | none |
| `slim` | everything 1 px | square | none |
| `rounded` | as chunky | the outer pixel of every right-angle turn removed (`round`, as `digitBody` does it) | none |
| `serif` | uprights 2 px at cap 7 and 9, 1 px at cap 5; bars 1 px | square | a 1 px foot each side of every upright's foot on the baseline, and of the cap-height head of I, J, T, and of the ascenders (b, d, h, k, l) |

Sizes (`FONT_HEIGHTS`, chosen by a 0..1 Size value as `digitSize` does: `min(2, floor(size x 3))`):

| Size | Cap height | x-height | Descender | Widest body | Space | Digit body width (chunky, rounded / slim / serif) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small | 5 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 2 | 5 / 3 / 4 |
| medium | 7 | 5 | 2 | 7 | 3 | 6 / 4 / 5 |
| large | 9 | 6 | 3 | 9 | 4 | 7 / 5 / 6 |

**Characters** (`FONT_CHARS`, in this order, which is the order of the frames of every font sheet): `upper` `ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ`; `lower` `abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz`; `digits` `0123456789`; `marks` `.,:;!?'"-+=/()%#*&` (18). A space is no glyph, only an advance.

**Where a glyph sits.** Every function places a glyph by its **pen point**: the left edge of its body and the **baseline** (the first row under the feet of the capitals). Capitals, digits and most marks stand on the baseline and rise `cap` rows; lowercase without an ascender rises `x` rows; b, d, f, h, k, l rise `cap` rows, t rises `cap - 1`; g, j, p, q, y and the comma hang below it (at most `descent` rows). Letters that would match a digit in shape alone (O and 0, I and 1, S and 5, B and 8, Z and 2, G and 6) are made one column wider or narrower than the digit, or given a different turn, so the pair never reads alike.

| Export | Signature | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `FONT_STYLES` | frozen list | `'chunky'`, `'slim'`, `'rounded'`, `'serif'` |
| `FONT_HEIGHTS` | frozen list | `5`, `7`, `9` (cap heights, small to large) |
| `FONT_CHARS` | frozen object | `{ upper, lower, digits, marks }` as above |
| `FONT_HAND` | frozen object | Hand-set glyphs by `'<style>/<cap>/<char>'` → rows of `#` and `.`; may be empty |
| `FONT_SAME` | frozen list | The letters, as `'<char>:<style>:<style>'`, allowed to come out the same in two styles because they have no turn to round and no foot to add (such as `'l:chunky:rounded'`); the test (4) reads it, so a reviewer sees every one |
| `fontMetrics` | `fontMetrics(size = 0.5, style = 'chunky')` → `{ style, cap, x, descent, stroke, bar, round, serif, space, track, maxW, digitW }` | The measures of one style at one of the three sizes (tables above); `track` is 1 (px between glyphs). An unknown style throws, listing `FONT_STYLES` |
| `fontGlyph` | `fontGlyph(ch, metrics)` → `{ body, w, h, rise }` | One character: `body` a `glyphGrid` of its body pixels (no rim, no ink), `w` and `h` its size, `rise` the rows above the baseline (so `h - rise` hang below). A character not in `FONT_CHARS` throws, naming it and listing the font's characters |
| `textBody` | `textBody(text, metrics, { track = metrics.track } = {})` → `{ body, w, h, rise, advances }` | A line of text as one `glyphGrid` on one baseline: glyphs `track` px apart, a space `metrics.space` wide; `advances` is the x of each character's pen point. Throws on an unknown character as `fontGlyph` does |
| `textWidth` | `textWidth(text, metrics, opt)` → number | The width of `textBody(text, metrics, opt)` without drawing |
| `drawText` | `drawText(g, text, x, base, { metrics, colour, outline = 'ink', edge, shadow = false, shine = false, offsets, ink = INK })` → `{ x, y, w, h }` | Draws a line with its first pen point at (`x`, `base`). `outline`: `'none'` (body only), `'ink'` (body and a 1 px `ink` line), `'rimmed'` (`rimmed` of `engine/ui.js`: body, a 1 px rim of `edge`, ink, holes inked). `shadow`: a 1 px ink drop shadow down and to the left. `shine`: the rows of each glyph above its middle row in the colour's light tone (`ramp(colour).light`). `offsets`: a list of whole-pixel rises, one per character (a bouncing word). Returns the box drawn, ink and shadow included. Draws nothing outside `g`; the caller keeps 2 px of room round the body for `ink`, 3 for `rimmed`, one more down and left for `shadow` |

**Test:** `tests/unit/pixel-font.test.mjs`. (1) For every character, style and size: the body is at most `maxW` wide; capitals and digits rise exactly `cap`; lowercase rises `x`, or `cap` for b d f h k l and `cap - 1` for t; g j p q y hang exactly `descent`. (2) Within one style and size, every pair of characters (aligned on pen point and baseline) differs by 2 or more body pixels, and the pairs O/0, I/1, l/1, I/l, S/5, B/8, Z/2, G/6 by 3 or more. (3) The mirror-symmetric glyphs (A H I M O T U V W X Y o v w x 0 8 - + = * #) are symmetric to the pixel in every style. (4) For every letter and size, any two styles differ by 2 or more body pixels, except the entries of `FONT_SAME`, and `FONT_SAME` holds no letter that does have a turn or a foot. (5) `fontGlyph` of each digit equals `digitBody(n, { w: digitW, h: cap, stroke, bar, round })` of `engine/ui.js` in chunky, slim and rounded, and that plus its feet in serif, except entries of `FONT_HAND`, which the test names. (6) `textBody` is as wide as its glyphs plus `track` between them plus `space` per space; `advances` increase; `textWidth` agrees; an unknown character throws with the character in the message. (7) `drawText` with `'none'` draws exactly the body pixels; `'ink'` adds only `INK` pixels round them; `'rimmed'` gives the same pixels as `rimmed(g, textBody(...).body, ...)` of `engine/ui.js`; `shadow` adds ink only down and to the left; `shine` changes at least one row of every glyph taller than 3 rows; nothing falls outside the returned box. (8) **A font sheet frame fits:** every character at the large size, `'rimmed'`, with `shadow`, drawn with its pen point at (4, 13) on a 16x20 surface, leaves row 0, row 19, column 0 and column 15 empty. (9) Same arguments, same pixels.

### H4b: `engine/counters.js` (new module): digit windows, rolling digits, a clock mark

Why it is needed: nothing draws a plate of digit windows, a digit rolling to the next one inside a window, or a clock picture. Imports `fontMetrics`, `fontGlyph` and `drawText` from `engine/pixel-font.js` (H4a, same batch: build H4a first) and `roundRectIn`, `SLICE` and `EDGE` from `engine/ui.js`.

| `clockIn` | `clockIn(cx, cy, r, { hour = 0.25, minute = 0 } = {})` → shape test | A small clock face: a ring 1 px thick and two hands from the centre (the hour hand shorter), the hands' angles from `hour` and `minute` (0..1 of a turn, 0 up) |

**Test:** `tests/unit/counters.test.mjs`. (1) `counterWindows` for `count` 3 to 8, every size and both `colon` settings: windows never overlap, keep `gap`, lie inside the box after `lead`, and are the stated size. (2) `counterPlate` in every style: every rectangle of `text` holds only `face`; `edge` is a pixel of the band; drawn twice with the same arguments, same pixels. (3) `rollDigit` at `k` 0 equals `from` drawn by `drawText` at the window's pen point, at `k` 1 equals `to`, at `k` 0.5 shows pixels of both, and never draws outside `win`. (4) `clockIn` with `hour` 0 and 0.5 differs by 3 or more pixels at `r` 5.

### H4c: `engine/emotes.js` (new module): marks over a character's head

Why it is needed: `engine/marks.js` has a 5 to 9 px `exclaim` and `question`, `engine/shapes.js` a heart, `engine/glyphs.js` a 10 px `sleep` status mark on a plate; nothing draws an anger mark, a sweat drop, a music note, a spark burst or a dizzy swirl, nothing sizes them to sit over a head, and nothing animates them. Imports `markIn` (`engine/marks.js`), `heartIn` (`engine/shapes.js`), `glyphGrid` and `rimmed` (`engine/ui.js`), and `fontMetrics` and `fontGlyph` (`engine/pixel-font.js`, H4a: the sleep Zs are the font's chunky `Z` and `z`).

| Export | Signature | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `EMOTES` | frozen list | `'exclaim'`, `'question'`, `'heart'`, `'anger'`, `'sweat'`, `'sleep'`, `'music'`, `'spark'`, `'swirl'` |

## Letters and numbers

### The font sheets

What all four share. One sprite per style; **every frame is one character**, drawn with `drawText` with its pen point at **(4, 13)**: the pivot. A sheet's rows are its four clips, and frame *n* of a clip is character *n* of that row of `FONT_CHARS`, so a game cuts the sheet into 16x20 cells, looks a character up by row and position, and lines glyphs up by their pen point. The cell is 16x20, not the style guide's 16x16 glyph frame (**a deviation**): the large size, rimmed, with its descender and shadow needs 17 rows, and the baseline must be at the same row in every cell. Glyphs are left-aligned at column 4, so a game that wants proportional spacing trims each cell's empty columns on the right and adds 1 px (the tracking); the blurb says so. **The clips are rows of pictures, not animations:** each plays at a fixed 2 fps for the playground, and there is no `speed` (a deviation from style guide section 10, which asks for `speed` on a clip of more than 4 frames: these frames are characters, not steps of a motion; say so in the report). No sounds. No colour control is named `face`, so the contrast test does not apply (nothing here carries text). At most 5 colours per clip (body, its light tone, rim, the rim's dark tone, ink).

## Left out, and why

| Wanted | Why it is left out | What would make it possible |
|---|---|---|
| Glyph widths and kerning in the downloaded JSON | Contract 8's sheet JSON has no per-frame width; a font sheet's cells are fixed 16x20 with the glyph left-aligned at its pen point, so a game trims empty columns itself | A `glyphs` entry in Contract 8 (`{ char, advance }` per frame), written by `describeSheet` from `fontGlyph`: a contract change, not a helper |
| Accented letters, other alphabets, symbols beyond the 18 marks | Each needs its own strokes and tests at three sizes; this round proves the method on the 80 characters every game needs | Rows appended to `FONT_CHARS` and the stroke tables, and a fifth clip per sheet, in a later part |
| A fifth, hand-drawn display style (outlined, inline, 3D) | Four styles cover body text, dialogue, cosy and storybook; a display face is a title, and the word pop's rim and shine already make titles | A style in `FONT_STYLES` with its stroke rule |
| A stone skin | Two stone looks exist: `ui-wood` style `stone` and the `gilded` skin's stone plate; a third would be a near copy | Nothing: crystal and grove are the new looks |
| Finishing wood, RPG and tech to four sprites each (the plan's "10 skins, 4 sprites each" is 28 of 40 after this round) | Wood has buttons and panels, RPG bars and a battle menu, tech bars and a card; their missing pieces are a different job (matching an existing look, not a new one) | A part 4: wood bars and controls, RPG buttons and controls, tech buttons, panels and controls, on `engine/ui-wood.js`, `engine/ui.js` and `techPanel` |
| A tail in the middle of a bubble's edge, drawn in one stretchable picture | A nine-slice repeats the middle of each edge, so a tail there would be repeated or cut | Already covered: the `tail` clip of `ui-speech-bubble`, placed by the game anywhere along the edge |
| Typewriter text, shaking or wavy dialogue text | Motion of the game's own text, not a picture | Game-side; `drawText`'s `offsets` serve a game that draws with the framework |
| Emote faces (smileys) and hand gestures | Faces are emoji designs a player names, and hands are body parts | Nothing for faces; a hand would need the hand shape part 2 also left out |
| A health strip under the name plate | A bar is its own asset kind with fills a game crops; the plate's `pointer` notch and a part 2 bar can be set together | A `plate-bar` clip once a thin bar (64x4) is in the style guide's frame table |

Sounds a row would have liked and that do not exist yet: a neon hum and buzz for the neon buttons (they cue the warm console set), a crystal chime set (they cue the glass set, which fits), a leaf rustle tap for the vine buttons (they cue the wood set), a speech blip for a bubble opening (`text-blip` is a voice, kept for the letters a game types), a snore for the sleep emote (it cues `notify-ping`).

## Batch plan

Each batch is one builder in one sitting, with review. **Numbering:** part 3 numbers its batches after part 2 (`core-sprites-2.md` used K11 to K20 and H1 to H3), so K21 to K27 and H4, H5 here never mean a batch of part 2. **Helper batches come first and build helpers only, with their tests, and no sprite.** There are two because the five modules fall in two independent groups: H4 is the text family (`engine/pixel-font.js` first, then `engine/counters.js` and `engine/emotes.js`, which import it), H5 the boxes (`engine/bubbles.js` and `engine/ui-skins-more.js`, which need nothing from H4). H4 and H5 run side by side. A sprite batch starts only when every helper batch it waits for is merged. **Pathfinders:** K21 settles how a glyph row is laid out, judged and exported, for every other sprite that draws text (K22, K24); K23 settles bubble tails, stretching and text areas for K24's balloons; K25 settles how a new skin is drawn over `flat` (the order of base, `roundSlab` and `moreSkinMarks`, and where the text area comes from) for K26 and K27. Their reports are read before the batches that wait for them. No batch waits on a sound batch: every cue is to a sound in `sounds/` today.
